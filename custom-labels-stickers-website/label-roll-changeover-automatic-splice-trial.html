Automatic Label Roll Changeovers: Plan the Splice Before Buying Rolls

A label roll that fits the spindle may still be unsuitable for an automatic changeover. Before ordering, ask the packer and equipment owner to specify how the old roll tail and new roll leader are prepared, and how the line will identify product affected by the join. A successful trial on the actual machine is the basis for deciding whether the proposed roll preparation is workable.

Separate a converter splice from a line-side changeover

A converter splice joins sections within a roll supplied by the label maker. A line-side changeover joins the expiring roll to a new roll at the packaging machine. They have different owners and may occur at different positions. An incoming roll can contain a converter splice even when a separate automatic changeover system is used. Agree how each is identified and handled; the incoming-roll splice guide addresses the first problem.

NJM describes an automatic label-splicing system for a particular installation. Its existence shows one possible equipment route, not compatibility with every labeler or roll. Obtain the exact splicer and applicator manuals and the line owner's instructions before fixing a roll specification.

Map the old roll, new roll and splice unit

Trace the web from each unwind through the join, sensor and dispensing edge. Mark which side carries labels, the unwind direction, core and maximum roll dimensions, label gap and web width. Ask the operator where the new roll must be staged and how much blank leader is needed for the specific equipment. Confirm the old roll tail, permitted joining materials, placement and any preparation that must happen before the run. Do not assume a universal leader length or tape type.

For an illustrative trial, suppose the line owner expects an automatic join while running a representative supplement label. The team first agrees the exact roll and machine settings, records the settings and prepares both rolls to that specification. This is a proposed test, not evidence that a particular line can run without stopping.

Agree preparation and affected-product handling

A blank leader or join can pass through a sensor and applicator differently from normal labels. Determine whether the line detects the join, pauses, rejects product, or requires an operator to identify and hold a defined interval. The line owner should establish that interval and the disposition procedure before testing. Check for missing, misplaced or damaged labels and reconcile held products against the line records. Do not release affected product merely because the splice has passed the dispensing point.

Assign the label supplier the documented roll preparation it has agreed to provide; assign the packer line setup, changeover operation and product disposition. Interfilling's delivery specification illustrates that a receiving operation may set its own label-roll delivery requirements; use the actual packer's requirements rather than importing another company's limits.

Trial a complete changeover with the packer

Run a representative old roll and incoming roll through the full sequence at agreed conditions. Record roll identifiers, preparation, target and observed speed, machine response at the join, sensor behavior, affected labels and products, operator interventions and final disposition. Review the record with the line owner against criteria agreed in advance. If the trial fails or automation is unavailable, document a controlled stop-and-change procedure and its owner before ordering.

For a custom supplement label, request a project quote with the splicer and labeler models, roll drawing, leader and joining requirements, target speed, stop constraints, quantities and trial contact. Ask which preparation and sample arrangements can be supported for this job.
