Tamper Evident vs Child Resistant: What Does Your Supplement Package Need?

Suppose a supplement buyer receives a quote for a seal that bridges the cap and bottle. The brief calls it a “security label,” while the product team asks whether it makes the bottle child resistant. Before approving the artwork or paying for production, resolve which requirement the package must meet. A visible opening cue and child-resistant access are different purchasing decisions.

If the requirement is evidence of opening, evaluate the proposed seal on the assembled package. If child-resistant packaging is required, obtain evidence for the identified packaging system through the responsible product and packaging team. A seal that tears conspicuously does not establish that the complete bottle meets child-resistant requirements. A project may need both functions, with separate evidence for each.

Separate the opening question from the access question

An opening seal asks: what can a person observe after the intended opening has disturbed this component? Child-resistant packaging asks whether the package meets the applicable access requirements, including adult usability. Neither a holographic appearance nor resistance to peeling answers that second question.

CPSC staff's Poison Prevention Packaging guide, page 17 explicitly distinguishes tamper evidence from special packaging. It explains that tamper features are removed before child-resistant testing unless they form part of the child-resistant design. The guide also states that CPSC does not approve or certify individual special packages.

That distinction changes what belongs in the purchase order. “Strong security sticker” leaves the intended function undefined. A more useful brief identifies the opening to be observed, the proposed component, and the separate status of any child-resistant packaging requirement.

Identify the complete combination before requesting evidence

Give the packaging team an identifiable assembly: bottle reference, closure reference, any liner or other sealing component, the proposed opening seal and the body-label location. Include the actual opening and reclosure sequence. A cap description such as “push and turn” is not a substitute for evidence covering the proposed combination.

Ask whether the available package documentation covers those components and the intended product. If a bottle, cap or another relevant element changes, have the responsible team determine which evidence remains applicable. A familiar bottle capacity does not establish that two assemblies are equivalent.

The label supplier needs a different, practical view of the same assembly. Show the surfaces the seal would contact, the movement it would cross and the information that must remain visible. A body label, neck band and cap bridge should be drawn together before their positions are fixed. Use the supplement label and tamper-band assembly guide if the components approach one another during packing or opening.

Ask for evidence that answers each requirement

Keep the two evidence requests distinct when comparing proposals:

Purchasing question Evidence to request What it does not establish
Will the proposed seal provide the specified visible opening cue? An identified seal construction, actual package sample and recorded observations under agreed opening and handling conditions Child-resistant performance of the complete package
Does the package meet an applicable child-resistant requirement? Applicability review and relevant protocol data or supporting package documentation, reviewed by the responsible product and packaging team Suitability of a separately selected printed seal or body-label layout
Can the label and closure work together in assembly? A representative assembled sample showing placement, opening access and information visibility Completion of either safety or opening-evidence approval by itself

A photograph can be useful evidence of a visible condition. It cannot stand in for a child-resistant test report. Equally, a package report does not demonstrate the appearance or opening response of a new seal that was never part of that proposal.

Confirm applicability for the actual supplement

Do not copy an over-the-counter drug packaging instruction into every supplement order. The CPSC guide discusses regulated substances and identifies dietary supplements containing iron among the covered categories, subject to the applicable provisions. Its discussion is not a determination that every supplement needs the same package. See the guide's scope and regulated-substance discussion, pages 7–9.

Provide the product's current formulation, presentation and intended market to the qualified reviewer. Ask for the applicable requirement and the evidence needed for that product, rather than asking the label printer to infer the answer from the front-panel artwork. Keep that decision with the package record. This article does not supply ingredient thresholds or a product-specific applicability ruling.

Use a split release when the evidence is incomplete

Consider a hypothetical supplement project, DEMO-SUP-A, with bottle B2, closure C4 and proposed seal S1. These are invented planning references; no sample has been tested.

The buyer has a seal drawing and the packer's proposed placement, but no recorded opening observation and no confirmed package-evidence reference. Procurement can request a conditional quote for S1, with the surface and opening requirements clearly stated. It cannot describe B2+C4+S1 as an approved child-resistant package.

Suppose a later seal trial records a visible tear. That observation may help the packaging team decide the opening-cue question under its agreed criteria. The child-resistant evidence and label-clearance questions still need their own decisions. Avoid a single “packaging approved” checkbox that erases the distinction.

The useful release names exactly what is authorized: for example, the artwork revision and seal location under review, or the complete component combination covered by the responsible team's package decision. An open dependency should remain visible when the order moves from a quotation to production.

Bring the two scopes into the label inquiry

For custom supplement labels, send the bottle and closure references, body-label dimensions, approved copy, quantities per design and the packer's assembly sequence. State whether the request concerns body identification, an opening cue or both. If a holographic seal is being considered, describe its intended physical function separately from its appearance.

Prepare a supplement label inquiry with package photographs, the proposed seal position and the status of the packaging evidence. Ask which label construction and sample scope can be proposed for the actual assembly. The quote should define the printed-label work while the responsible team retains the decision on complete-package requirements.
