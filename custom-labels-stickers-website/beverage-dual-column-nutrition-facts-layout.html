Will Your New Beverage Bottle Need Dual-Column Nutrition Facts?

A larger bottle can keep the same recipe and still need a different Nutrition Facts panel. Before asking a designer to make the old artwork fit, have the person responsible for nutrition labeling confirm how the new retail unit must present a serving and the contents of the whole bottle. If two columns apply, give the designer both approved sets of values and measure the space they actually occupy.

Start with the bottle that will be sold

Give the regulatory reviewer the product category, net contents, package configuration and market. Specify whether the bottle will be sold individually. If a multipack is also planned, identify that configuration separately. A drawing of the bottle establishes its shape. The net contents specification establishes the quantity of beverage it will hold.

Three quantities can appear in this conversation. Net contents describe what the buyer receives. A reference amount customarily consumed, or RACC, is the category-specific reference used to determine a serving size. The serving size on the panel describes the basis for its nutrition information. FDA explains that serving size reflects customary consumption. A reader looking for what they would consume by finishing the bottle needs information on that whole-container basis. FDA explanation of serving size

FDA's guidance treats an individually packaged and sold beverage below 200% of its applicable RACC as a single-serving container. The whole bottle is the serving. From 200% through 300%, inclusive, the guidance describes a requirement for per-serving and per-package columns, subject to exceptions. Limited labeling space can matter under specific provisions. Ask the reviewer to record which provision applies to this exact bottle. A nominal bottle size alone leaves the product category and any applicable exception unresolved. FDA serving-size guidance, sections A and C · FDA summary of the guidance

What the designer needs from the nutrition reviewer

Request one dated data file or approved panel for the new SKU. It should identify the serving size, servings per container, the column headings and all final nutrient amounts and percentage Daily Values that belong in each column. Identify which product and fill specification that approval covers. If the source is a spreadsheet with several flavors, name the tab and revision in the handoff.

An old flat proof may show a rounded per-serving value. Multiplying that printed value by the number of servings can carry the rounding into the whole-container column. Refer the calculation back to the nutrition data owner, who can work from the underlying data and return the display values. An instruction such as “double the old column” leaves the designer making a nutrition decision each time the underlying numbers change.

Illustrative change order. A beverage brand has approved art for a smaller bottle and a dieline for a larger one. Its reviewer confirms that the larger bottle needs two columns. The team can now distinguish two reasons a proof might be held.

Required column Approved data reference Available width Layout decision
Per serving Smaller bottle proof only Measured on new dieline Obtain the new SKU's approved serving data
Per container No approved whole-bottle values yet Same shared panel Reserve room for both columns and hold numeric typesetting
Both columns New SKU's complete approved panel received Compare full panel with usable width Fit at actual size or revise the label area

The first two rows describe the same unfinished job. The last row shows what changes when the data arrive. No nutrient values need to be invented to decide what work can proceed.

Fit the complete panel on the finished wrap

Place the approved panel at its intended print size on the measured dieline. Include the longest heading, the widest value and the full bottom of the panel. Mark the wrap overlap, the boundary of adjacent copy and any area that the container shape makes difficult to read. Print a paper mockup at actual size and wrap it around a representative bottle. Check whether a reader can follow one nutrient row across to the correct column heading.

If the second heading lands in the overlap, move optional copy or test a wider usable label area. If the artwork team proposes a different presentation, return it to the regulatory reviewer with measurements and the complete proof. The small-bottle layout guide illustrates how to document a space deficit on a wrap. Its worked example concerns a supplement bottle. Small-bottle panel layout guide

Finally, compare the bottle name, fill size and any front-label serving references with the revised back panel. Give the new size its own proof identifier so that a later reorder of the smaller bottle points to the correct artwork.

For a print inquiry, send the measured labeling area, bottle drawing, approved panel revision, quantity by SKU and the specific fit question you want a proof to answer. Include both bottle sizes if the distinction matters at packing. Discuss a beverage label proof
