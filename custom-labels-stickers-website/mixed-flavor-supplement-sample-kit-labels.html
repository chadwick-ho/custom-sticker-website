Mixed-Flavor Supplement Sample Kits: Keep Carton and Sachet Identities Aligned

A mixed-flavor kit is not one enlarged sachet design. The carton and each inner unit need a matching identity and a clear sale-unit decision.

Published October 16, 2026 - By RP Labels

A three-flavor trial kit looks like one friendly box on a shelf. At the artwork desk, it is at least four identity decisions: the carton and three separate inner sachets. If a buyer sends only the box design, I ask what happens when somebody takes one sachet out. Can a warehouse worker, a retailer and the end user still tell citrus from berry without guessing?

The pictured RP Wellness kit is fictional and deliberately not a legally complete supplement pack. It demonstrates flavor separation and pack structure. Actual ingredient, Supplement Facts, identity, net quantity, claims and business details must be approved for the intended market and sale format by the brand's qualified reviewer.

Start with the unit that is sold

Is the sealed carton the only retail unit, or will individual sachets be offered separately? Will a distributor break kits into samples? The answers change the information plan. The FDA's dietary supplement labeling guide describes required statements and where they generally belong. Its small and multiunit package guidance also shows why pack configuration matters. Neither source makes a short sachet automatically exempt. Ask the brand's regulatory reviewer to decide what each sale unit needs before dielines are locked.

If the kit is the only retail unit, the carton may carry much of the consumer-facing information, subject to the applicable rules. Inner units still need enough operational identity to prevent flavor and lot mix-ups in the business's workflow. If sachets can be separated for sale, that assumption needs a fresh legal and artwork review, not a slightly larger flavor icon.

Build one matrix before designing four faces

Make a table with rows for carton, citrus, berry and mint. Columns should include product identity, flavor, net quantity, lot or date print zone, barcode or internal code, required copy owner, artwork revision and approval status. Add a column for whether the text is fixed print or variable overprint. The matrix catches the error that a beautiful carton mockup hides: one inner flavor might contain a different formula or quantity.

Visual color is useful, but never the only identifier. Print the flavor name in readable text and use a simple original icon. A color-blind operator, a black-and-white packing checklist or a low-light shelf photo still needs a way to distinguish the units. The SKU and flavor guide goes deeper on ID control.

Proof the packing operation, not only the flat art

Put full-size printed samples of the sachets inside a production-size carton. Check whether the front panel truthfully describes the count and mix. Check that seals, folds and tear notches do not remove essential inner text. Then run a simple pick-and-pack exercise: place one flavor from each artwork revision into a kit and have a second person verify the contents against the matrix. Any ambiguity at that table will become a costly packing exception later.

Keep the carton and sachet version numbers linked. When a flavor changes, decide whether the carton count or flavor list also changes. Do not let an old carton remain in stock because only the inner art was revised. The stick-pack and carton hierarchy guide covers copy placement; this kit guide adds the multi-flavor release decision.

What the buyer should request

Send the exact pack count, all flavor names and formulas, market of sale, whether inner units can be sold or handed out separately, carton and sachet dimensions, sealing equipment, quantities per design and who signs off the final regulated copy. Request one version-controlled proof set rather than four unrelated PDFs. If one sachet is not approved, the kit is not approved.

I would pay for an extra controlled proof round before paying for a carton full of mismatched inner units. The box is the promise; every sachet has to keep it.
