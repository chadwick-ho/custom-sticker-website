Label PDF Page Size vs Finished Cut Size: Check the Right Boundary

Do not change a label quote to match the PDF viewer's largest page measurement until the designer and converter identify which boundary represents the agreed finished cut. A page can include bleed, white space or printer marks outside the label. A die-cut shape also needs its own approved path rather than a guessed rectangle.

For a small-vial buyer, confusing those objects can turn a correct artwork file into the wrong requested size. Keep the finished specification, exported PDF geometry and physical paper-proof scale as three related checks.

Read the boundary names before comparing numbers

Adobe's page-box guidance distinguishes CropBox, TrimBox and BleedBox. TrimBox describes the final page after trimming; BleedBox covers area beyond trim for professional printing. The media/page extent can include space outside those boundaries. Have the designer or prepress owner report the relevant dimensions from the actual export, with units and file revision.

In this invented rectangular example, the outer page is 64 by 44 mm, the bleed rectangle 54 by 34 mm, and the finished rectangle 50 by 30 mm. Those dimensions can coexist without describing three alternative label orders. The illustration uses 2 mm beyond the finished edge solely for its arithmetic; obtain the real supplier allowance.

The geometry demonstration PDF contains that constructed example. It is not production artwork, a tested dieline or a converter preset. Its boxes illustrate the difference; they do not establish that another supplied file uses the correct boundaries.

Keep a cut path distinct from a rectangular page box

For a rounded or irregular die-cut label, identify the actual path and its finished width/height with the converter. A rectangular TrimBox by itself does not specify every corner radius or contour. Conversely, changing a viewer's visible crop does not create the intended cut path or add missing bleed artwork.

If the page measurement differs from the quote, ask the designer to show the named page boxes and the approved dieline together. If the exported metadata is wrong, correct the export with its owner. If the finished cut itself changed, return that size change to the buyer and converter before treating it as the same order.

Check paper-proof scale without resizing the approved file

Adobe's print guidance distinguishes actual-size output from fit-to-page or custom scaling. A reduced printout can look balanced while giving the buyer a misleading bottle wrap.

At 75 percent, a 50 by 30 mm rectangle becomes 37.5 by 22.5 mm on the illustrated output. That is multiplication, not a recorded office-printer result. For a fit rehearsal, identify the intended actual-size setting and measure the paper output against the agreed dimensions. An instruction to print at 100 percent is not evidence that a particular printer did so.

The dieline/bleed guide covers content clearance and cut tolerance after these objects are identified. A paper wrap remains a geometry aid, not an adhesive or color approval.

For vial labels, prepare the inquiry with the actual container, released finished dimensions, units, PDF and dieline revisions, quantities and the named boundary causing the disagreement. Ask the converter to confirm that geometry before revising the quote.
