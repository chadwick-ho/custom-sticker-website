How to Test Glare on Glossy Supplement Labels

A supplement packer needs to keep a glossy bottle design, but the line camera sometimes rejects a correctly printed lot code. Before buying a matte label or changing the camera recipe, compare both variables on the filled bottle. Keep the glossy finish if an approved lighting setup brings false rejects within the line team's limit and still catches the specified defects. If the glare persists around one field, trial a matte inspection area before committing the whole label to a different finish.

Find out what the camera is rejecting

Pull the rejected bottle and save the original camera frame and inspection result. Check the lot text with an independent reference, such as the approved lot string and a direct look at the print. Under controlled conditions, observe whether the bright patch moves as the viewing or lighting angle changes. A print flaw follows the same spot on the label. A changing highlight is a reason to test the lighting. When a seam or a rotated bottle hides the field, establish a workable view before running a finish trial. Applied camera view and orientation guide

Name the inspection task before you change anything. Reading a date, finding a missing character, and checking whether the lot string matches the job can fail in different ways. For each task, mark the field on the artwork and record the active inspection region and decision settings.

Cross two lighting setups with two finishes

Start with the glossy production construction and one candidate the converter can identify and sample, such as gloss with a matte patch around the lot field. Run both finishes under the current lighting and under one alternative the line team can install and reproduce. An angled light can move a reflection. A diffuser can change how much of the curved surface appears bright. Crossed polarizers are another option to evaluate. They reduce transmitted light, so check exposure and motion blur at operating speed and look for any loss of contrast on the defects the camera must catch. Basler machine vision illumination guide · LUMIMAX guide to polarization filters

Keep the bottle revision, fill state, artwork, lot-marking method, application position, camera geometry and inspection task constant. Use matched samples on the actual bottle, with representative rotation and line speed. Record the light position, exposure and recipe for each condition. Use the same exposure and decision settings where feasible. If exposure must change to make a candidate usable, document and retest that combination as a production recipe.

Run known good bottles and known defects

Have quality identify the samples independently of the camera before the trial. Include approved prints and agreed defects that challenge the actual job, such as a missing digit and a legible wrong lot string when the system checks the expected value. Use controlled trial samples and keep them out of saleable stock. Preserve sample IDs and vary run order where practical so a shift in ambient light or line conditions can be traced.

Log each sample by task and trial condition. For known good samples, record passes and false rejects. For known defective samples, record detections and missed defects. Use separate totals for the good and defective groups and retain representative frames and decision logs. A setting that rejects fewer good bottles can also let defects through. Ask quality to set limits and trial coverage before reviewing results, including any worst-case bottle orientations and relevant line conditions.

Approve the finish and the inspection recipe together

If gloss works at normal production speed, release it with the proven light and recipe settings. If a local matte area is the better candidate, check its boundary against both the mark position and camera field across normal application variation. Review how the patch looks on finished bottles and check the lot mark on that surface. Trial full matte as its own construction if the local patch fails the inspection or appearance checks. Keep a reference bottle and the approved recipe with the release record so a later finish or lighting change can be reassessed. Supplement label finish guide

For a custom supplement label, share the bottle revision and a physical bottle, current finish construction, marked artwork, lot-marking method, camera and light models, line speed, and the sample conditions quality will run. Include quantities by version, delivery location and your sample needs. Ask which local or full matte constructions can be quoted, then have the line team approve the applied trial.
