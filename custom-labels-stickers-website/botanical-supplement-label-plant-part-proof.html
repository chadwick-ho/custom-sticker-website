Botanical Supplement Labels: Check Plant-Part Copy on the Print Proof

A botanical name and its plant part can be right in the approved copy and wrong on the wrap proof. If the proof reads “leaf” but the approved copy for that SKU says “root,” hold the proof and send the mismatch back to the copy owner. Don't choose the wording based on the old label, the space available, or what appears on a supplier's general product sheet.

Record the approved wording and where it belongs

For U.S. dietary supplement labels, 21 CFR 101.4(h) is the starting point for the common or usual name of botanicals, including fungi and algae, and the statements that follow it. Have the brand's regulatory reviewer confirm the exact text and placement before prepress. For each botanical line, keep these items in the proof record:

  • Common or usual name. The label name must be consistent with the names standardized in the incorporated 1992 edition of Herbs of Commerce. Match the proof to the brand reviewer’s approved name for that ingredient. A marketing name on the front panel is not the copy reference for this check.
  • Plant part. Check that the printed proof matches the approved plant part in English. The rule does not require the plant-part designation for algae; that exception is not a reason to skip the rest of the identity review.
  • Latin binomial. When required, check the full binomial, spelling, and parentheses. Follow accepted nomenclature rules; include authorship where needed for positive identification. The required Latin name can appear before the plant-part statement. The exception is tied to the Latin name being available in the incorporated reference for the common or usual name actually used on the label. A current web listing or another edition does not establish that exception. Record the brand reviewer's determination; don't infer that a particular binomial can be dropped.
  • Panel and listing. Identify the panel and line approved for the SKU. 21 CFR 101.36(d) and (d)(1) link source-ingredient identification to § 101.4 whether the source appears in the nutrition label or in the ingredient statement. Don't move a line or drop it as a supposed duplicate without the copy owner's approval.

Section 101.4(h)(3) allows a placement choice for a single-ingredient dietary supplement whose label has no ingredient list. The plant part and, when needed, Latin binomial can be shown prominently on the principal display panel or information panel, or included in the Supplement Facts nutrition label. Record the selected placement. This provision neither establishes that your SKU can omit its ingredient list nor gives the same placement choice to every product with a single named botanical.

Compare the approved source line to the actual print proof

  1. Start with the approved copy revision, the dieline revision, and the exact SKU and package size. Use one record per botanical line: expected name, part, binomial or documented reviewer decision, location, and proof ID.
  2. Compare the text on the exported artwork character by character. Then inspect a full-size print proof and apply it to the intended container or a dimensionally matching mock-up. Check that the plant part and parentheses remain visible, stay associated with the right line after wrapping, and are not obscured by curvature, trim, or overlap.
  3. Mark each row as matched, mismatched, or awaiting review. For example, if a line break separates the approved plant part from its botanical so that it reads as part of the next entry, flag the layout even when no text is missing. If artwork is revised, recheck the affected text and neighboring lines on the new proof before release.

Keep the copy approval and the print approval separate. A matching proof shows that approved wording survived production artwork and layout; it does not establish regulatory compliance. For related checks on ingredient statements, see the ingredients and allergen panel proof guide.

Keep the plant part attached to its own source row

In a dense panel, finding all the expected words somewhere on the label is insufficient. Identify each botanical row in the copy source, then follow its approved part statement and any required binomial through the exported layout. A nearby line break or seam can make a retained word appear to belong to a different entry.

Have the copy owner mark the association on the exact proof revision. If the layout leaves the relationship ambiguous, return that proof to the designer without guessing a new ingredient name or moving the statement to an unapproved panel. Recheck both the changed row and its neighbors at finished size. Send the marked reference with the supplement-label inquiry, alongside the approved copy and container drawing.

Request a print quote against the approved copy

For custom supplement labels, send RP Labels the approved artwork and copy revisions, a marked copy-to-panel map, container dimensions and dieline, stock and finish, and quantities by SKU. Flag any unresolved copy or placement questions for your regulatory reviewer before releasing artwork. Keep the reviewer decision with the proof version rather than asking the printer to choose the botanical wording.
