Tamp-On or Blow-On Bottle Labels: Proof the Transfer Step

For a print-and-apply system using a tamp or blow-on pad, approve the label through the complete transfer route. The sample should leave the liner, reach the pad in the intended position and reach the bottle correctly. Ask the packer to identify its actual applicator and transfer mode before ordering a production roll.

A bottle label that works on a roll-on applicator may need a different review on a pad-based system. The decisive question is where the candidate first stops behaving as intended.

Name the applicator as well as the printer

Give the converter the printer model, applicator model, pad reference and proposed label dimensions. Include the shape and condition of the bottle area being labeled. Have the equipment owner state whether the bottle is stationary or moving at transfer and whether the pad contacts it.

Cab's print-and-apply overview distinguishes roll-on, blow-on and tamp-on application. Its label-quality recommendations explain the need for media that separates and handles correctly in its systems. Use the requirements for your installed equipment and configuration when preparing the order.

The material brief should identify the finished face, adhesive, liner and protective finish. Ask for a trial in the intended shape and size. A larger rectangular label can interact with the pad differently from a small shaped label, even if the supplier describes the material in the same terms.

Observe three transfers before changing the construction

What the packer observes What to record and review
The label remains partly on the liner or leaves it inconsistently Retain the strip and roll identity. Ask the converter and equipment owner to review cut quality, release and the installed dispense arrangement.
The label leaves the liner but arrives skewed or insecure on the pad Identify the pad and the label face presented to it. Have the equipment owner review the pickup with the actual shape and finish.
Pickup is repeatable, but the label lands poorly on the bottle Record the bottle position and transfer event. Have the equipment owner review the transfer and the material owner review the proposed bottle bond.

These observations help route the next investigation. They do not diagnose a cause from a photograph. Keep the same label revision and bottle reference while the owners investigate the first observed difference. The liner comparison guide covers a proposed liner change in more detail.

A glossy, embossed or otherwise changed face should return to the pad review if that face is presented to the pickup. Ask the equipment owner what the current pad can accept. A material name or a successful peel by hand cannot answer that question.

Run the sample in the intended sequence

Agree a trial scope that includes normal loading, starts, stops and relevant bottle variation. The equipment owner sets operating parameters and acceptance criteria. Record the sample roll, configuration, observed transfer stages and rejected specimens. Retain a correctly applied bottle and representative failed samples where practical.

For a stable bottle and a candidate that completes all three stages, the team can review remaining purchase differences such as price, supply and the agreed inspection scope. If the target placement depends on a tightly controlled bottle position, close that equipment question before committing to stock. Changing adhesive alone leaves a positioning problem unresolved.

Check the finished bottle after the agreed handling interval too. Transfer success and the later bond answer different questions. Keep the approved sample with the roll requirements so a subsequent finish substitution returns to the appropriate review.

Request labels for the installed transfer route

Prepare an RP Labels inquiry with the bottle, artwork, per-design quantity, complete applicator reference and the identified sample requirement. Ask which finished label construction and trial stock can be quoted for the packer's review. Include its approved roll dimensions and orientation.

For supplement labels, state who applies the label and who approves the finished-bottle result. That connects the print order to the actual transfer route.
