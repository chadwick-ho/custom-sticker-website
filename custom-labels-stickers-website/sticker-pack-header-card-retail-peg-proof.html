How to Proof a Sticker Pack Header Card on a Retail Peg

Take the approved bag of stickers to the retailer’s hook before ordering the printed header card. A hole can slide onto the hook in a flat card sample, yet sit too low once the card folds over a filled bag. Approve the assembly shoppers will handle: hang it, lift it off, put it back and check which artwork they can see.

Start with a packed bag and its place on the display

Use the approved contents and the bag the packer will actually use. Record the bag’s flat width, its thickness and weight when loaded, the closure at the top, and the space from the top edge to the first sticker. If the contents shift upward when the bag tilts, mark how far they move. That determines where a fastener could reach a sticker.

Get the retailer’s hook reference and a sample of the intended fixture for the trial. A photo or dimensioned sketch helps the printer select a candidate opening. The hook tip, neighboring packs and available space above the hook all affect how the shopper lifts a pack away. Confirm the final choice on the fixture. HSPack identifies bag width, attachment and hook fit as inputs to header-card selection. HSPack header card specifications

Mark four features on the printer’s template

  1. Card and printed faces. Identify the card stock and template revision. Mark the front seen on the peg and the back read in the hand. Set the pack name where it remains visible with the card folded.
  2. Hook opening. Trace the proposed opening on the folded sample. If the die cuts through both panels, check that the two openings line up after attachment and that the bag top leaves the path clear.
  3. Fold. Fold the dummy on its score over the loaded bag. Check where the lower edge falls against the window of visible stickers. Ask for a revised fold or card depth if it hides the artwork the shopper needs to see.
  4. Attachment area. Mark where the packer will staple or otherwise secure the header. Keep the pack name, essential artwork and stickers clear of that area. With staples, inspect the reverse and the bag contents after fastening. StickerApp shows cut, safety and staple guides for its header templates. StickerApp header artwork guide

Hang, lift and replace the finished sample

Have the packer attach a sample using the proposed stock and method. Load the hook the way the retailer expects to merchandise it. View the front pack at shelf distance, remove it and return it without moving the fixture. Does the header tip to one side? Can a shopper clear the hook tip without snagging the next bag? Look for a hole stretching or tearing, a card pulling loose, blocked copy or a fastener touching the stickers.

Record the hook reference, bag and header revisions, attachment method, observation and decision. A tilted pack calls for another look at the hole position and balance of the loaded bag. A face hidden behind the header calls for a change to the fold depth or artwork. Mark the change on a photo and repeat the same handling step. Keep one signed, loaded sample with the purchase order.

Specify what arrives at the packing bench

Ask for separate lines for flat printed and die-cut cards, empty bags, and the labor to bag the contents and attach the header. State who supplies each item, where components meet and whether the buyer receives loose parts or finished retail units. A price per printed card and a price per assembled pack cover different work. If the printer also quotes the sticker pieces, define their backing and cut format separately. Sticker cut and delivery formats

Send RP Labels the packed sample reference, bag width, loaded thickness and weight, hook drawing or photo, header copy and versions, attachment method, order quantity and packing owner.
