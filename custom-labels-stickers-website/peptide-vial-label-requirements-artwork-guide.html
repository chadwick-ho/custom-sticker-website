What to Put on Peptide Vial Labels: Artwork and Proof Checklist

A good peptide vial label does not try to say everything. It identifies the bottle quickly, keeps important fields readable and gives the buyer a clean way to separate versions, batches or product lines.

The hardest part of a custom vial label is not always printing. It is deciding what deserves space. Small bottle labels become messy when buyers try to include a full website, long warnings, multiple icons, tiny QR code, batch text and a large logo on one narrow label.

The core fields most vial labels need

For custom research-use vial packaging, the most useful fields are simple and practical. They help identify the bottle, organize production and support repeat orders.

Brand or logoKeep it clear, but do not let it take all the space. A small vial needs identification first.
Product name or codeThis should usually be the most readable line on the label.
Batch or lot fieldUseful for version control, packing records and customer reorders.
Strength or variant fieldOnly include fields that are relevant to your product category and market.
QR code or barcodeUseful for tracking or product pages, but it must be large enough and placed on a calm background.
Color systemUse color bands, cap-matching colors or side strips to separate similar bottles.

Avoid claims you cannot support

Packaging should not make unsupported health, treatment or performance claims. Buyers are responsible for checking the rules in their own market and product category. If a project connects to supplements or regulated products, review official requirements before printing final copy.

For dietary supplements in the United States, FDA guidance and 21 CFR 101.36 explain Supplement Facts labeling requirements. A peptide vial project may be a different product category, but the same principle applies: do not guess compliance copy. Confirm the category, market and intended use before printing.

Artwork files that make production easier

Vector artwork is best when possible. Send AI, PDF, EPS or SVG for logos and sharp text. PNG or JPG references are useful for style direction, but they may not be enough for final print if the resolution is low. If your logo is only a screenshot, RP can still review it and tell you what needs to be rebuilt.

Artwork checklist before proof

  • Logo file, preferably vector
  • Exact label text, including product name, code and batch fields
  • Bottle size and label size, or bottle photo if size is not confirmed
  • Material choice: white, clear, matte, gloss, holographic, BOPP or PET
  • Color references or existing brand colors
  • Roll direction if labels will be machine applied
  • Any QR code, barcode or serial number requirements

How to make a label look professional

Professional vial labels usually have fewer design elements, better spacing and stronger hierarchy. Use one main type style, one clear product name area and one supporting information area. If you need premium effect, use material and finish, not a crowded layout.

For a product line, build a consistent system. Keep the same logo position and label structure, then change color, code, icon or side band for each version.

Proof review: the step buyers should not skip

The proof should be checked at actual size. Zoomed-in artwork can hide problems. Look at the final label width, small text, QR code, edge gap, color contrast and whether the label still feels readable once wrapped on the bottle.

FAQ

Can I send only a reference image?

Yes. A reference image helps with direction, but final production may still need rebuilt logo or text files for clean printing.

Should the label include a QR code?

Use a QR code when it has a real purpose: reorder page, authentication page, product information or batch tracking. Do not add it if it makes the label unreadable.
