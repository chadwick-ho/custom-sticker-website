Custom Label HS Classification: Describe the Material and Imported Form

Before asking for an HS or HTS classification of a label shipment, describe what is actually being imported: its materials and construction, whether it is labelstock or finished labels, its form and dimensions, and its intended function. Do not copy a code from another supplier merely because both invoices say stickers.

For a U.S. import, give those facts to the importer and its customs adviser. The supplier can provide the actual manufacturing description; it should not infer the classification from the bottle artwork or promise a duty result without the applicable review.

Separate face material from the release liner

A self-adhesive plastic label can arrive on a paper release liner. The backing and the material that remains on the bottle do different jobs. A statement such as paper-backed labels can leave the reviewer unsure which component the seller means.

Ask for the actual face material, adhesive, liner and any relevant coatings or layers. Include their composition or other detail the reviewer requests, using supplier documents rather than inferring material from appearance. A clear-looking film is not a complete chemical or construction description.

The material-document guide helps identify evidence to request. A datasheet can support a factual description; it does not itself allocate a customs code to the finished shipment.

Describe the form at the border, not only its later use

Identify whether the goods are unconverted stock, blank labels, finished printed die-cut labels or a different item. State rolls or sheets, actual width and other relevant dimensions, printing and cutting state, and what is packed with them. Do not describe a roll of printed labels as bottles or supplements simply because those products appear in the artwork.

Description field Useful factual input
Components Actual face, adhesive, liner and relevant layer description
Imported state Stock versus finished labels; printed/blank and cut/uncut as supplied
Physical form Rolls/sheets, dimensions and how finished pieces remain on the liner
Function and shipment Actual labeling use, goods included, origin information and destination

These fields prepare the review; they do not settle every classification question. Mark unsupported facts as awaiting the supplier's confirmation instead of selecting an apparently favorable description.

Use published rulings as scoped references

CBP's April 2026 paper-label ruling N359904 addresses specifically described printed gift labels under heading 4821. Its November 2025 ruling N355268 addresses described self-adhesive vinyl stickers under heading 3919. Those are different products reviewed on their submitted facts, not RP products or blanket codes for every paper or plastic label.

Do not transfer their detailed codes or historical duty statements to a new order without the applicable review. A material, form, use or other relevant difference can matter. The purpose of the comparison is to show why the word sticker alone is inadequate, not to classify your shipment through a two-row rule.

CBP's Binding Ruling Program describes obtaining a pre-entry decision with the relevant product information. Where certainty is needed, the importer or its authorized adviser can consider that route for the actual goods. This guide submits no ruling request and states no current rate, exemption or origin outcome.

Put the accepted description back into the shipping handoff

For custom supplement labels, prepare the RP inquiry with the construction, finished format, quantities and destination, plus the importer's requested factual fields. Ask the supplier to identify any description it cannot yet support. Retain the responsible classification decision and its scope with the shipment records; use the invoice/packing-list guide to keep those documents consistent with the packed goods.
