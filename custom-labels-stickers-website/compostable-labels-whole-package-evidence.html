Compostable Labels: Check the Whole Package Before Making a Claim

A compostable face stock is a starting point, not evidence for a compostable finished package. The buyer needs to know what the proposed claim covers, which components remain attached at disposal, and whether the intended composting route can handle that particular assembly. Hold the artwork if the evidence only covers a material sample and the claim describes the whole package.

Map the claim to the finished assembly

Write the exact proposed words and where they appear: on the label, package, website or disposal instruction. List the package material and each label layer, including adhesive, ink, coatings and any laminate. Record component versions and suppliers. Identify whether the label is expected to remain attached. A certificate or technical sheet for one layer should identify its scope and conditions; it does not automatically establish the performance of the printed, applied assembly.

The FTC Green Guides summary discusses the need for competent and reliable scientific evidence and qualifications for compostability claims, including the conditions and facilities involved. Review the actual wording, audience and applicable rules with the claim owner before approving copy.

Build an evidence coverage matrix

Question Record to request Decision if missing
What is claimed? Exact artwork wording and whether it refers to the label or whole package Hold or narrow the claim
What is covered? Face stock, adhesive, ink, coating, substrate and the finished combination, with document versions Identify uncovered components and request assembly evidence
Under what conditions? Test or certification scope, composting environment and documented limitations Do not infer home composting from an industrial test
Where will it go? Intended collection and facility acceptance for the destination market Verify the route and qualify instructions as appropriate

For example, a supplier sheet covering only the unprinted face stock leaves the adhesive and printed assembly unresolved. Even a complete assembly report does not itself establish that local collection accepts it. Record each gap and its owner rather than marking the package approved from the first document received.

Separate route, access and other environmental claims

Home and industrial composting are different routes. Confirm what conditions the evidence actually covers and whether the facility or program accepts the finished package. A laboratory result does not establish universal access to a collection program. Review the consumer instruction and facility availability in each intended market; local requirements can differ.

Carbon emissions and PFAS content are separate questions requiring their own evidence and claim review. Compostability evidence does not establish either. For a different end-of-life decision, see recycled content versus recyclability.

Prepare the inquiry

Send the label team the package substrate and size, label construction and artwork, expected disposal route and markets, exact claim, existing component and assembly documents, application conditions, quantity and required performance. Ask what constructions and documentation can be supplied for review; product suitability and any certification remain to be confirmed. For supplement packaging, the supplement label page describes a relevant print inquiry, not a compostability certification.
