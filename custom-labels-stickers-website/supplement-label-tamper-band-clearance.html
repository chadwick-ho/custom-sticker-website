Supplement Bottle Label Clearance: Caps, Shoulders and Tamper Bands

The bottle may look cylindrical in a product photo, but the usable labeling area ends before the shoulder begins and before the base starts to turn. That quiet gap prevents a surprising number of production problems.

The short answer: size the pressure-sensitive body label to the bottle's true straight wall, then leave a controlled gap from the shoulder, base radius and tamper band. Confirm the gap on physical bottles because catalog dimensions, molding variation and application equipment all affect the usable area.

One of the most expensive phrases in packaging is "use all the available space." The artwork gains a few millimeters. The label edge moves onto a curve. The front panel looks generous on screen and starts wrinkling on the line.

At the factory, the bottle does not care how badly the marketing team needed one more icon. It changes diameter where it changes diameter.

Find the straight wall first

Place the bottle upright and identify where the shoulder becomes parallel and where the lower wall begins to curve into the base. The vertical distance between those points is the starting label height, not the full visible body height.

A supplier drawing is useful, but keep physical samples. Bottles sold under the same nominal capacity can have different shoulder profiles, panel recesses and molding tolerances.

Why shoulder contact creates wrinkles

A flat pressure-sensitive label wraps a constant-diameter cylinder cleanly. At the shoulder, the circumference becomes smaller. The label must either wrinkle, bridge away from the surface or distort.

Softer film can tolerate a little more shape than stiff paper, but material flexibility is not permission to design onto a compound curve. Shrink sleeves are better suited when true 360-degree coverage over changing geometry is required.

Buying rule: choose the label format from the bottle geometry. Do not ask pressure-sensitive material to behave like a shrink sleeve because the artwork needs more height.

Tamper bands need their own operating space

A clear or printed shrink band moves and tightens during application. It also tears, cuts or peels when the customer opens the bottle. Keep the body label out of that working path unless the structure was intentionally designed and tested as one component.

If the band catches the body label edge, it can wrinkle the label during shrinking or lift it during opening. If the body label hides the perforation, the tamper feature becomes harder to understand.

Cap diameter is not bottle diameter

Do not use the cap as a visual ruler. Wide caps, induction-lined closures and child-resistant closures can overhang the neck. Measure the label panel directly with a flexible tape or calculate circumference from the straight-wall diameter.

For machine application, also note guide rails and bottle handling points. A label that clears the shoulder may still collide with a fixture or sit too high for the applicator wipe-down system.

Leave the base radius alone

Labels placed too low can wrinkle at the bottom edge, pick up scuffing from conveyors or appear crooked because the base ring is not perfectly parallel to the artwork. A small controlled margin usually looks more deliberate than a label forced to fill the body.

I have seen buyers reduce that margin because the bottle looked "unfinished." After filling, the product line added a date code below the label and the bottles traveled through a conveyor guide. The empty sample had not told the whole story.

Prototype the complete closure stack

Test the bottle with the production cap, induction seal where relevant, tamper band, body label and filled weight. Apply and shrink components in the intended order. Then open the package as a customer would.

Photograph the approved clearances and record the label size. This reference is more useful during a reorder than a note that says "same as last time," especially when a bottle supplier changes molds.

Clearance approval checklist

  • Physical production bottle matches the quoted bottle
  • Straight-wall height is measured between both transition points
  • Label edge stays off shoulder and base curves
  • Tamper band shrinks without catching the body label
  • Perforation and opening path remain visible and usable
  • Applicator guides and wipe-down rollers clear the label position
  • Date coding and conveyor contact areas remain available
  • Approved bottle, label and closure photos are archived

FAQ

How much space should a label leave below the shoulder?

There is no universal gap. Measure the actual straight wall and test the final construction with real bottle tolerances.

Can a body label touch a tamper band?

Avoid uncontrolled contact. The band can wrinkle the label during shrinking or lift it during opening.

Why does a label wrinkle near the top?

It is often entering the shoulder radius, where the bottle circumference changes.

What dimensions should be sent to the printer?

The supplier drawing, straight-wall height, circumference or diameter, shoulder and base transition points, closure dimensions and physical samples.
