How to Count and Cut Transfers on a UV DTF Gang Sheet

Two quotes can offer the same sheet dimensions and give your application team different amounts of work. One supplier may deliver the whole printed sheet. Another may price separate transfers that an operator can pick up and position one at a time. To compare the quotes, ask for a layout that shows how many copies of each design fit, then agree who separates them and what the operator receives.

Work back from the items being decorated

For each design, give the finished width and height, approved revision and quantity needed on finished items. Mark whether rotation is acceptable and identify designs that must stay together in a fixed relationship. Two words aligned as one bottle logo, for example, may need to travel on one piece of carrier. Counting those words as separate transfers creates a positioning job at the application bench.

In an illustrative order, a buyer needs 40 large emblems called A and 20 small marks called B. A proposed sheet holds four A and two B. Ten sheets supply 40 A and 20 B on paper. This calculation assumes every planned position yields a usable transfer and allows nothing for trials or replacements. List those separately. A trial that consumes two A and two B before production changes the requirement to 42 A and 22 B. Eleven copies of this layout would then print 44 A and 22 B.

Question for the illustrative order Design A Design B
Needed for finished items 40 20
Positions on one sheet 4 2
Printed on ten sheets 40 20
Remaining after two trial applications per design, from those ten sheets 38 18

If the layout changes, redo the arithmetic by design. An apparently spacious alternative with five A and one B on a sheet would need 20 sheets to reach the original B requirement under that single layout. Eight sheets would cover A alone. A second layout or a short run of B might make more sense. Ask the supplier to show the quantities that would actually ship before judging the price per sheet.

Draw the cuts on the proposed layout

On a proof copy, mark the outside sheet boundary, the proposed strip or piece boundaries, and the artwork that must remain on each piece. Make it clear which marks are cutting instructions and which are printable artwork. Check whether the operator can separate a piece without crossing an adjacent graphic, pick it up with its carrier intact and see the alignment features needed for placement. Agree on the handling area for the proposed materials and cutting method.

Roland describes gang printing and applying transfer film to the printed sheet in its UV DTF workflow. Its instructions apply to its specified components. Get the practical spacing and handling dimensions for your supplier's system before locking the layout. Packing artwork edge to edge in a file establishes a visual fit. It leaves the person cutting and applying it to discover whether the individual graphics can be handled. Roland's gang-sheet and transfer notes · Roland transfer system workflow

Choose what reaches the application bench

A whole sheet suits a buyer who can separate designs and keep track of counts. Specify the orientation and include a layout map with the shipment. Strips can keep repeated marks in order, but the applicator needs to know which design is in each strip and who makes the final separation. Individual transfers can be counted by part ID at receipt. Define the carrier area and how similar sizes or revisions will be distinguished in the package.

If separation will happen at a different site, name that site or party in the quote. Include who supplies the cut map, who counts the separated pieces and how incomplete sheets or damaged positions will be recorded. The receiving team may verify a carton of sheets long before the application team can verify usable pieces. A separate count at each handoff avoids double-counting replacements. Reconciling shipment and label quantities

Before committing the layout to the full order, ask for a mixed-sheet trial if one is available. Have the intended operator separate both the largest and smallest designs and position each on its actual surface. Record which design and position were involved if a cut leaves too little carrier to handle. A cut-map revision might solve that issue without revising the logo itself.

Send RP Labels the final-size designs, quantity per design, target objects, rotation restrictions and desired delivery unit. Include the proposed layout if you have one and identify who will cut and apply the transfers. Ask for the per-design count and any separation work to be stated in the quote.
