2 Inches or 50 mm? Resolve a Label Quote Size Change

A 2-inch label and a 50 mm label are different finished sizes. If a quotation replaces one with the other, treat that as a proposed specification change. Ask for an exact-size offer and a clearly identified alternate, then approve the alternate only after the bottle fit and artwork have been reviewed.

This matters when a supplement brand sends an inch-based drawing and receives a metric quotation described as the same size. Purchasing may see an equivalent; prepress and the packer need one unambiguous cut dimension. An unnoticed substitution can leave the order, artwork and accepted bottle mockup describing different labels.

Convert the number before comparing the offer

NIST defines one inch as exactly 25.4 mm. Therefore, a 2 by 2 inch finished cut is 50.8 by 50.8 mm. A 50 by 50 mm proposal is 0.8 mm smaller in each overall dimension. These calculations describe nominal geometry; they do not set a manufacturing tolerance.

RFQ or offer wording What it establishes What purchasing should do
Finished cut: 2.000 by 2.000 in Exact conversion: 50.8 by 50.8 mm Ask the supplier to quote this cut and state the proposed production tolerance
Alternate finished cut: 50.0 by 50.0 mm A different proposed size Review it as an alternate, with its own drawing and approval
Approximately 2 in; metric dimensions to follow No released size yet Resolve the controlling dimensions before artwork approval

For a wrap label, a width change affects the intended gap or overlap. A height change affects the space between the label and the bottle shoulder or heel. If you keep the top-left corner aligned on a drawing, the difference appears at the right and bottom; if you center the artwork, it is distributed differently. Agree the reference position rather than assuming the whole difference belongs at one edge.

Identify which dimensions the quote describes

Ask whether the quoted width and height are the finished label cut, the artwork including bleed, or the release-liner width. They are separate fields. A correct unit conversion on the wrong field still produces a wrong order.

The label dieline guide explains cut, bleed and safe area. For the purchasing record, attach the marked drawing with its filename and revision and state which unit controls. If you show both units, derive the second from the same value. Do not make the supplier choose between contradictory inch and metric dimensions.

A useful clarification is: Please quote the finished cut at 2.000 by 2.000 in, equivalent to 50.8 by 50.8 mm, against drawing A revision 3. If you propose 50.0 by 50.0 mm instead, list it as an unapproved alternate and return a revised dieline. This is sample wording, not a released RP specification.

Compare exact-size and alternate-size quotes fairly

There are two reasonable routes. Keep the original cut when it belongs to an approved packaging specification, a repeat-order reference or a constrained bottle application zone. Evaluate the alternate when the brand is willing to reopen that dimension and the supplier has explained what changes in the offer.

Ask what motivates the alternate: proposed tooling, stock availability, layout or another constraint. Request the actual effect on price, tooling charges and schedule in writing. A nearby metric size is not automatically cheaper or faster, and this guide does not confirm that RP holds either die.

Keep quantity per artwork, material, adhesive, finish and supply format the same in both quote lines wherever possible. If the alternate also changes a material or roll specification, identify that difference separately. Otherwise the price comparison cannot tell you what the dimensional change buys.

Reapprove the fit and layout before accepting the alternate

  1. Packaging owner: check the proposed cut on the actual bottle at the intended application height. Use the supplement bottle size guide for the fit method and record the changed gap, overlap and edge clearance.
  2. Designer or prepress: return the layout on the alternate dieline. Review border placement, copy clearance, barcode space and any reserved batch-printing field. Do not shrink the entire artwork merely to make it fit.
  3. Packer: confirm whether the changed cut also changes label pitch, dispensing or registration requirements. The finished cut does not define all roll dimensions.
  4. Purchasing: obtain the revised drawing reference, proposed dimensional tolerance and explicit approval from the responsible owners. Update the order and quote together.

A paper fit check resolves geometry, not adhesive performance. If the alternate changes the construction or application conditions, carry those changes into the relevant sample review as well. Stop approval if the quote still says 50 mm while the proof says 2 inches, or if an alternate drawing has replaced the original without a recorded decision.

Send one resolved size brief

For custom supplement labels, prepare the actual container reference, controlling cut dimensions and units, drawing revision, pieces per artwork and intended application method. Mark any alternate size as pending until reviewed. Use RP Labels' contact page to prepare your enquiry and ask whether the exact cut and a separately quoted alternate can be evaluated for your project.

The enquiry should leave the supplier with one clear question: quote the approved size, or propose a documented change for review. It should not ask production to interpret an approximate conversion.
