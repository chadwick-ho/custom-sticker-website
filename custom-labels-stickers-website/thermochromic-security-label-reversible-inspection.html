How to Specify a Reversible Thermochromic Security Label

Consider a reversible thermochromic mark if your receiving team can deliberately trigger it, observe a distinct change and watch it return to its baseline on the finished pack. Get the proposed temperature range and procedure from the supplier, then try them in the rooms where staff will use the mark. If ordinary storage or handling already triggers it, or the change is hard to see under normal inspection lighting, hold the purchase until the design or inspection conditions are resolved.

Decide what the inspector needs to learn

A reversible mark can provide a secondary visual cue during a check. It can return to its original appearance after the trigger is removed. The EUIPO describes reversible and irreversible thermochromic effects and notes that some inks become clear and reveal the artwork underneath. Schreiner Group describes one example of a reversible reveal at a defined temperature change. Ask the specialist what the proposed print and finish should show. EUIPO guide to thermochromic inks · Schreiner Group's semi-covert feature examples

If staff need visible evidence that a package has opened, assign an opening-evidence requirement to the actual seal and opening path. If they need to know what happened to the shipment earlier, specify the temperature record separately with the quality owner. A mark that resets can look the same before and after an earlier temperature event. Record a matching response as one observation in the inspection workflow and route discrepancies to the brand owner. Visual authentication and QR checks · Temperature indicators and data loggers

Agree on what three states look like

Ask the supplier for a reference on the actual substrate with the intended background print, backing and finish. For a reveal design, use a clearly identifiable shape or pattern on the reference. Test the mark on the pack because the background visible through a clear region, or glare on the finish, can change what the inspector sees. Keep a matched comparison sample without the responsive feature where available.

Put the room conditions, light source, viewing angle and distance on the inspection record. Ask the supplier to specify the permitted activating and recovery conditions and propose observation windows for that construction. Use a separate line for each state:

  • Before activation. Record the sample and reference IDs, how the pack was conditioned, and the mark's expected starting appearance and location.
  • During activation. Apply only the supplier's agreed stimulus. Record the method and conditions used, when the change becomes observable, and which shape or artwork the inspector can identify.
  • After recovery. Remove the stimulus and return the pack to the agreed conditions. Record whether and when the expected baseline returns. Note a partial change, slow recovery or an ambiguous observation for review.

Try the approved check where staff will use it

Suppose a mark is easy to read on a backed sample at a receiving desk, but barely visible against the actual pack under lighting at the return station. Bring the same applied sample and identified reference to both stations. Compare the controlled check under each station's usual lighting and agreed ambient range. If daily handling might accidentally activate the mark, observe a representative pack through that handling route and record whether it arrives at the expected baseline.

Put the inspection procedure and the reference revision on the same record. If the reference itself fails to show the agreed states, have the inspection owner resolve the conditions or replace the reference before judging a shipment. If the reference works and the observed pack differs, record the pack and lot and follow the brand's escalation procedure. Repeat relevant observations if the substrate, ink, finish, backing or inspection location changes.

Ask which construction can be quoted

Send the package drawing and substrate, planned mark location and backing, finish, conditions at storage and inspection, the person who checks the mark, and the expected baseline, active and recovered appearances. Include any separate seal requirement, quantities by version, and the samples or reference needed to approve the check. Ask what thermochromic work, if any, can be quoted for the actual construction.
