Proprietary Blends on Supplement Facts: Check the Print Proof

Suppose a U.S. supplement brand has signed off the proprietary-blend declaration for a bottle label. In the returned PDF, the designer has moved the blend amount below the name to fit the panel and placed an ingredient from the separately declared section under the blend. Both changes need to be caught before the label goes to press.

Release the proof only after the designer maps every affected line to the signed declaration and the brand’s regulatory reviewer approves the final layout. Send a shifted amount, changed ingredient position or mismatched footnote back for correction. If the approved material cannot fit on the available label, take a revised layout or packaging plan back through review before issuing the print order.

Start with a signed declaration for the exact SKU

Have the brand’s regulatory lead issue artwork-ready copy with the SKU and revision, serving basis, approved blend name, per-serving blend total and units, and the ordered list of blend members to print beneath the name. The same reviewer confirms which dietary ingredients must be declared separately under 21 CFR 101.36(b)(2), the approved entries for those lines, and the symbol and footnote. The designer needs the approved text and order for comparison. Keep the formulation and internal calculations with the brand.

Map each approved line to the print proof

Use a markup sheet with one row per item below. Record where it appears in the PDF and mark it matched or held for correction. The checks follow the proprietary-blend provisions of § 101.36(c), including the separate treatment of (b)(2) entries and the placement of the blend total and symbol. Current 21 CFR 101.36(b)(2), (b)(3) and (c)

  1. Separate dietary ingredient lines. Confirm that any blend ingredients subject to (b)(2) are declared as specified under that paragraph. Check each separate entry against the signed copy, including its approved amount and any applicable % Daily Value.
  2. Blend name and weight per serving. Match the approved blend name. Confirm the total is on the same line to its right, aligned under the applicable amount column. The blend total under (c)(3) covers the weight of the blend’s other dietary ingredients under (b)(3). Check the approved value and units without recalculating from the PDF.
  3. Members under the name. Compare each approved (b)(3) member in sequence. The rule calls for descending predominance by weight and indentation beneath the blend name. The members can appear in a column or linear list as applicable. In a linear list, read across line breaks to catch dropped or transposed names.
  4. Symbol and footnote. Under (c)(3), put the blend’s symbol under the % Daily Value heading if present, or immediately after the blend weight if the heading is absent. Match it to the same symbol at the bottom of the nutrition label with the statement “Daily Value not established.”

FDA’s nutrition-labeling guide discusses the proprietary-blend declaration in Question 34. Use the current regulation for the exact symbol placement and the illustration reference in § 101.36(e)(11)(v) for one example of the presentation. FDA Dietary Supplement Labeling Guide, chapter IV

Review the final-size proof on the actual bottle

Print the PDF at finished size and apply the mockup to the intended bottle. Follow the eye from the blend name to the amount, through the member order and down to the footnote. Check the trim, overlap and curve for clipped names or symbols and shifted alignment. If the PDF needs a different panel format or footprint, have the brand’s reviewer approve the revised presentation. Use the small-bottle Supplement Facts guide for the space decision and the ingredient and allergen panel guide for other copy affected by a formula change.

Keep the signed declaration, exact proof revision and approval together. If the serving basis, blend name, total, member order or print copy changes, route the affected proof back to the brand’s regulatory reviewer before release.
