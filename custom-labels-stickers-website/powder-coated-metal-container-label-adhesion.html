Labels for Powder-Coated Metal: Approve the Actual Finish

Suppose a buyer is sourcing powder-coated tins in a smooth finish and a textured finish. The proposed label looks secure on the smooth sample. During a trial on the textured tin, an edge lifts and a colored fleck comes away on the label. The buyer needs to know whether to quote another label construction or hold the tin lot for review.

Approve one label construction across both finishes only if the agreed trial supports each finish code on actual cured production parts. If a lifted sample appears to have taken coating with it, hold the affected lot from label release and give the tin supplier both sides of the separation to inspect. If the coating appears intact, have the label supplier and application owner review the construction, contact area and application conditions before the next comparison.

Identify the cured surface at the label footprint

Ask the tin supplier for the part revision, external coating or finish code, lot reference and the finish state at the exact label location. Note whether the landing area is flat, curved, textured or interrupted by a seam. Agree which cleaning or surface preparation the supplier permits. Include the normal time or condition between cure and label application if the coating owner considers it relevant. This check applies to labels applied to the exterior after the finish is cured.

Label Aid describes how coating, texture and surface condition affect adhesive contact on metal parts. Marking Systems describes its experience with constructions for powder-coated and textured finishes. Use that guidance to request samples matched to the finish in the purchase order. Label Aid guidance on labeling metal surfaces · Marking Systems guidance on powder-coated surfaces

Run the finish comparison under matched conditions

Apply the same identified printed construction at the same footprint on both finishes, with the application method, preparation, conditions, dwell and observation points agreed in advance. Retain an unlabeled companion tin of each finish and lot for visual comparison. Use a sample of the identified approved finish as a labeled reference when one is available. Record differences in geometry or application so the result can be interpreted against the parts actually tested.

Check edge condition and contact after the agreed handling sequence. If a second label construction needs a trial, run it on the same identified finish alongside the first under matching conditions. Record whether the facestock, finish or footprint also changed. Keep any required numerical test method and acceptance criteria with the person responsible for the specification. See the guide to comparing peel, tack and shear data.

Inspect the label underside and the coating patch

Photograph the label underside, the spot on the tin and the unlabeled companion together. Identify the tin and label samples in the record. Use the appearance to route the issue for follow-up.

  • Coating looks intact. Record where the label lifted and whether residue appears on the tin or label. Have the label supplier and application owner review adhesive contact, application and label construction.
  • Colored material on the label and a changed patch on the tin. Record both observations as possible coating involvement. Have the tin supplier or coating owner determine which layer separated and whether a coating test is needed. Save the parts for that review.

If a coating adhesion method is required, the coating owner or qualified lab selects and interprets it. ASTM D3359 describes a controlled tape method on cuts in certain coating films on metal. An ordinary label peel supplies a visual observation for the investigation. ASTM D3359-23 scope

Release by finish code and request a quote

Record the approved label construction and footprint, covered coating codes and lots or part references, application conditions, sample IDs and release owner. If only the smooth finish meets the agreed checks and the buyer can use it, release that finish code and keep the textured option pending review. If the finish changes again, review which evidence still applies before accepting it.

For a custom supplement label project, ask RP Labels to quote against the identified tins. Send dimensions and artwork, cured finish codes, photos or available tin samples, permitted preparation, application and use conditions, quantity per design and any observed failure. Ask which constructions and sample comparisons can be scoped for the exterior label.
