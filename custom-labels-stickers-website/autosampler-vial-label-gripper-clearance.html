Autosampler Vial Labels: Keep the Gripper Area Clear

When a research vial enters an autosampler, obtain the equipment's vial and label requirements before releasing the label cut. Confirm the permitted label band and the maximum applied build on the actual vial, including its seam. A readable code on a loose bottle leaves mechanical handling to be checked.

The laboratory's equipment owner should identify the sampler model, vial, closure and tray configuration. Use that exact set when deciding which sample the label supplier needs to reproduce.

Put the equipment-defined label area on the drawing

Ask the equipment owner for the applicable manual revision and vial reference. Mark the permitted label area and any area that must remain free of label or tape. Record the required position relative to a fixed vial feature rather than relying on an operator's description of the middle.

Agilent's 7693A sampler manual includes a label-placement drawing. Its 7890-series troubleshooting manual asks users to check label size and interference with the gripper when investigating a mishandled vial. Those are named equipment examples. The dimensions for another sampler must come from its own accepted specification.

If the available label area is too small for the intended copy, have the laboratory decide which information belongs on the vial and which can be retrieved through an approved record. Keep the human-readable and code requirements explicit. Reducing text on the screen does not settle whether the instrument can handle the labeled vial.

Measure the finished build at the overlap

Give the converter the label construction proposed for the actual application. Identify the face, adhesive and any laminate or covering step. Ask how the finished sample represents the intended build.

At a seam, two overlapping layers can produce a larger local build than the rest of the wrap. Mark that location on the applied specimen. Have the equipment owner check the assembled vial against the applicable limit and handling envelope using its agreed method.

Specimen condition What to resolve
A gap or single layer throughout the permitted band Confirm the finished labeled wall and edge position on the actual vial.
An overlap within the permitted band Check the maximum local build and the overlap position, as well as the ordinary wall.
A laminate, overlabel or replacement added later Return the changed specimen to the envelope review before using the earlier approval.

Keep skew and raised edges visible in the review. A nominally correct rectangle can extend into a restricted area after application. The small-vial gap and overlap guide helps describe the intended seam, while the machine owner determines whether that seam suits its handling arrangement.

Let the laboratory review the complete handling path

Arrange the trial through the authorized equipment team. Provide the identified vial and label specimens in the state that the agreed evaluation requires. Ask the team to record loading, pickup, transfer and return as applicable to its normal procedure. Supplier approval of a flat label cannot stand in for that equipment acceptance.

Record the barcode result separately. A code can read correctly while the vial catches during handling, and a smoothly handled vial can still present its code poorly. The tray-scanning guide addresses optical access where that is the unresolved task.

If handling fails, keep the sample identity and observations with the equipment review. Have the responsible owners determine whether label position, build, vial variation or another factor needs a change. Repeat the relevant check on the revised specimen.

Send the permitted envelope with the quote request

For custom vial labels, supply the actual vial, equipment reference, permitted label drawing, approved artwork, quantity and application method. Include the required sample form and the laboratory contact who will review it.

Prepare the RP Labels inquiry with the equipment-defined restrictions attached. Ask which construction and physical sample can be quoted against them, then retain the accepted specimen and drawing for reorders.
