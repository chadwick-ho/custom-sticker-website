How to Proof a Bottle Neck Hang Tag on a Capped Bottle

The strap of a promotional neck tag can sit neatly on a bottle neck and still catch on the cap on the way down. Before committing campaign artwork to a die, give the person who will fit the tags a capped sales bottle and a blank cut with the printer’s proposed die. Choose the hanger that person can fit at the agreed packing step, with the message facing the shopper and the bottle artwork visible.

Start by deciding where the tag goes on. A hanger fitted before case packing has to travel beside other bottles and dividers. A tag fitted by store staff needs an instruction they can follow with the bottles already on hand. A brewery keg collar uses a different fitting and identifies a keg. For a piece that must adhere across the closure and body, use the neck-seal proof guide.

Follow the opening over the finished closure

Bring the bottle as it will be sold, with the actual cap, any overcap or band, and the approved bottle label in place. Mark the widest part the opening must clear and the point on the neck or shoulder where the hanger should rest. Ask the printer for the die reference, a flat drawing that identifies the print faces, and a cut-and-scored dummy in the proposed stock. Keep the dummy with the drawing so a later change in die or stock is visible.

Dilco shows several necker shapes and supplies separate artwork templates for one- and two-sided pieces. NTX illustrates how a score positions the printed panel below the opening. Use the template for the shape you are trying on the bottle. Dilco bottle necker templates · NTX scored bottle neck tag

Lower the dummy over the cap as the packer would. Watch for a torn edge, a folded tab that stays bent, or contact that shifts the closure or its band. If the tag needs to be forced, mark the point of interference on the dummy and send a photo to the printer. Try the revised die on the same finished closure before approving the opening.

Look at the bottle from the shelf and from the case

Write the campaign headline on the intended face of the dummy. Set the bottle on the shelf with its main label facing forward. Lift it, turn it as a shopper might, then put it back. Where does the headline settle? If it hangs over the flavor name, decide whether to shorten the flap or move the score. Printing both faces could help with a tag that turns over, but it cannot uncover wording hidden underneath the flap.

For tags fitted at the plant, place the bottle among its neighbors in the intended case and divider. Close the case normally, reopen it and remove the bottle. Photograph the resting face, any crushed corner and any spot caught by a divider. For store fitting, try the sequence from opening the case to facing the shelf and pack the loose tags where store staff can find them.

Give the packer one approved reference

Keep the signed sample and two labeled photos, one from the shopper’s viewpoint and one showing how to put the tag on. The fit record should identify the bottle and closure, die revision, stock, print side or sides, visible face, location of attachment, person fitting the tag and case or display used. Mark the outcome as approved for that combination or return it with the specific change to try.

For a project inquiry, include the capped bottle photos and dimensions, die reference, case layout, attachment stage, campaign versions and quantity by version.
