Metallic Supplement Labels: Metal Detector Review

Do not approve or reject a supplement label for a metal-detection line from its silver appearance alone. Identify the actual label construction, then have the packer's inspection owner review the finished package on the intended inspection system. Thin metallized film, a foil layer and a metallic-looking printed effect need distinct material descriptions; appearance does not establish their inspection behavior.

The buying decision is whether the proposed package can meet the site's inspection requirements. A label that looks right but has no inspection decision should remain a candidate. An empty bottle passing through a detector without a rejection is not evidence that the system will detect the required contaminants in the finished pack.

Replace “silver label” with a material description

Ask the label supplier to identify the face material, decorative transfer or ink, any metal-containing layer, and its coverage in the finished label. Request available material references for the inspection owner. A color swatch or digital rendering is insufficient for that handoff.

Keep these descriptions separate:

Description in the proposal Information still needed for inspection review
Metallic appearance Which material or printed effect creates it, and whether that construction contains metal
Metallized film Exact film construction, metallic layer information available from the supplier, and coverage
Foil or foil decoration Whether this means a foil layer or a transferred decorative construction, with the actual material and area identified

The foil versus metallic-ink guide addresses the appearance comparison. Here, retain the material identity even where printed colors conceal the reflective layer. A white background over a metallic construction changes its appearance without establishing that the underlying metal has been removed.

Put the whole package and inspection point in the brief

Record the actual sequence of filling, closing or sealing, labeling and inspection. State which components are present when the package reaches the detector. Include the contents, bottle, closure, seal and any other relevant packaging, rather than asking the equipment owner to assess a loose label in isolation.

If inspection occurs before label application, make that fact visible to the quality owner. It does not automatically settle the suitability of the overall inspection plan. If inspection occurs after labeling, the candidate label belongs in the package assessed at that station. Purchasing should document the existing sequence, not move the inspection step to make a preferred finish appear acceptable.

Identify the detector model and the owner of its approved program. Provide the accepted reference package, if one exists, and explain exactly what the new proposal changes. This makes it possible to assess the label change without losing track of changes to the contents or other components.

Treat metallized film and foil as separate equipment questions

LOMA's guide distinguishes inspection of products in metallized film from inspection of foil-packed products and relates system selection to the application. That distinction supports requesting an equipment-specific assessment; it does not mean every metallized label will pass or that every foil-containing package has the same inspection options. LOMA guide to metal detection.

METTLER TOLEDO describes its Profile Advantage equipment for applications including products packed in metallized film. This is a named equipment example, not compatibility evidence for a different detector or your supplement bottle. Profile Advantage brochure overview.

The practical consequence is to send the actual construction and package to the site's equipment and quality owners. They should establish whether the existing system is suitable and what evaluation is required. A universal “metal-detector-safe” description attached to the label would skip that work.

Ask for the decision behind the trial

Have the packer arrange assessment of representative finished packs through its approved procedure, involving the equipment supplier where needed. The trial record should identify the reference and candidate packages, contents and component revisions, actual inspection location, operating conditions and procedure used.

Ask the inspection owner to record the conclusion on the required detection and rejection functions. The owner sets the test materials, methods and criteria; purchasing should not invent a test-piece size or interpret a quiet machine as a successful validation. Record any scope that the trial did not cover.

Keep compatibility and contamination decisions separate. If a pack is rejected, follow the site's established handling procedure. Do not classify it as a harmless label response simply because the new artwork is metallic. Likewise, an unrejected pack cannot, on its own, demonstrate contaminant-detection performance.

Do not lower sensitivity or skip inspection to obtain a favorable decoration result. If the candidate is unsuitable, ask the relevant owners to review another label construction or a formally assessed inspection approach. An appearance change can be proposed by purchasing; acceptance of the safety controls belongs with the responsible team.

Release the identified package, then control changes

Approve the label for this package only when the inspection owner has documented acceptance of the applicable scope and the order matches the reviewed construction. Hold it when the metallic layer is unidentified, the sample lacks a production component, or the decision addresses only appearance.

Keep the record compact: label construction and coverage; other pack components; station and equipment; trial reference; decision owner; change conditions. A new metallic coverage area or material substitution should return to the owner for a decision on further review. Inspection acceptance also leaves artwork readability as its own task; the Facts and barcode neutral-zone guide covers that requirement.

For custom supplement labels, provide the desired appearance, proposed construction, container and closure, actual inspection point and the packer's technical contact. Include quantities, required sample format and any alternative appearance the brand could consider.

Prepare a construction-specific label inquiry and ask RP Labels to confirm the available proposal, supporting material information and sample scope. Send those inputs to the packer's inspection owner for the package decision. A label quotation does not replace that acceptance record.
