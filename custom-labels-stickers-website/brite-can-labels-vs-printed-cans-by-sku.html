Brite Cans or Printed Cans? Plan Inventory by Flavor

A seasonal flavor loses half its fill slot after the packaging arrives. Can the core flavor use the spare cans? With a brite can (an undecorated can), it may be able to, up to the point when someone applies the flavor label. With a can printed for the seasonal SKU, the next fill needs to match the artwork already on the can.

Build a can-allocation sheet before comparing prices. A dependable repeat run is a candidate for dedicated printed cans if the brand expects to use the quoted quantity while the artwork is current. For a flavor whose dates or quantities move, ask the filler which other runs could actually use the blanks. That answer determines how much flexibility a label buys.

Get the filler to identify the shared stock

Give each SKU a row for each fill date. Enter the unit count, spare allowance supplied by the filler, can body and end references, liner specification, artwork revision and packing site. Ask the filler to initial the rows that can draw from the same stock. Two cans of the same stated capacity can have different specifications.

Record where and when the label goes on. If a decorator labels a full pallet before shipment, those bodies are already flavor-specific by the time the filler receives them. If the filler accepts blanks and applies labels as needed, the unassigned bodies may still be available when the schedule changes. Get both the can specification and the decoration route confirmed before counting stock as shared. For examples of the two commercial routes, see Belmark’s can decoration overview and TricorBraun’s beverage packaging brochure.

Recount the stock after one fill changes

Suppose flavor A stays at 8,000 units and flavor B falls from 2,000 to 1,000 after ordering but before any decoration of blanks. Both runs use the same filler-approved body, end and labeling route. For this example, assume no opening stock, no scrap or spares, exact order quantities with no supplier minimum, one body and one end per unit, and one label per unit on the brite route. All 9,000 revised units are filled.

Component ordered Original order Used on revised fills Left after both fills
Shared brite bodies 10,000 9,000 1,000 undecorated bodies
A labels 8,000 8,000 0
B labels 2,000 1,000 1,000 B labels
Alternative: bodies printed for A 8,000 8,000 0
Alternative: bodies printed for B 2,000 1,000 1,000 B bodies
Common ends, on either route 10,000 9,000 1,000 ends

Compare the two body orders separately. The brite route leaves enough bodies and ends for a compatible later 1,000-unit fill. An A fill would also need 1,000 new A labels. The unused B labels can wait for another approved B run. The direct-print route leaves enough printed bodies for 1,000 more B units, provided the artwork is still usable. Replace every example quantity with the supplier’s actual order increment and the filler’s allowance before costing either route.

Put an owner beside every leftover

Suppose the purchase order transfers ownership to the brand on delivery. On the brite route, the filler holds the brand’s 1,000 spare bodies and 1,000 ends, and the decorator holds its 1,000 B labels. On the direct-print route, the filler holds the brand’s 1,000 B bodies and 1,000 ends. In this example the brand authorizes reuse or disposal. Your contract may assign ownership differently.

Give each remaining component a separate line on the purchase order. Name the owner, storage site, person who inspects the stock, party paying storage or disposal, and person who can release it to a later run. Ask both parties to confirm whose inventory ledger carries each balance and who pays if a new B design retires the old artwork.

Compare landed cans and ends, labels, application labor, changeovers, freight and storage against the same fill calendar. Ask which party ships each component and whether the filler accepts buyer-supplied cans in that condition.
