Align a Supplement Bottle Label to a Molded Logo or Seam

When a supplement bottle has a molded logo that must line up with the center of its label, agree on a bottle reference and a target position before you order printed rolls. Ask the packer how the applicator locates that reference. If the artwork works at any angle and the rest of the pack has no fixed front, specify that it can rotate freely and remove the orientation requirement from the quote.

Put the bottle and label references on the same drawing

Start with an actual bottle from the container specification. Circle one repeatable reference in a photo, such as the centerline of an embossed logo. If a seam is proposed, identify which seam the operator should use. Two similar seams can be easier to see than they are to tell apart.

Draw the bottle viewed from the cap end. Mark the starting reference, the direction in which angles increase, and the label feature that must land at the target. It might be the center of the label's front panel or the centerline of a printed stripe. Keep the nominal position and the accepted range on the same revision. The brand and packer agree that range from the design and line trial.

The KHS camera-system description gives seams, embossing and lettering as examples of features its system can use. It is an example of machine capability. Give your own operator the bottle, the proposed reference and the equipment model so they can confirm whether that setup can reproduce it.

Match the approval to the way the bottles are applied

Project condition What to approve
The mark and label must align for a hand-applied order A locating jig or repeatable hand-placement method. Retain applied bottles showing how the operator finds the reference and checks the result.
The mark and label must align on a repeat line An equipment-specific trial with the proposed bottles, finished roll and normal line settings. Ask the packer to record its orientation method and agree what to do with missed readings.
The label can land anywhere around the body An applied sample at several allowed positions. Check those positions against any remaining constraints, such as a barcode near a rib or a graphic across a seam.

For a line trial, have the container supplier or packer identify the bottle lots actually used. Have the operator record whether the reference was located and where the label landed on the same specimen. A feature that looks distinct when held under a desk light may be harder for a particular sensor or camera to see on a moving bottle. If the first article looks right but the line misses or confuses the reference, ask for a different reference or an orientation-tolerant layout before signing off.

A label can be correctly spaced on its liner and still land at an unexpected angle around the bottle. Use the liner position guide to check the roll dimension. When ordering a two-label set, also specify the applied gap between the front and back labels.

Send a bottle-relative placement brief

Prepare an RP Labels quote request with the bottle reference, marked top view, target label feature, quantity per design and any roll requirements from the packer. If the bottle must be indexed, include the operator's agreed placement range and trial plan. Ask which supplement label construction and roll format RP can quote against that plan.
