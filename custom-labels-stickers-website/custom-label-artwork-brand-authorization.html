Ordering Labels for Another Brand: Who Releases the Artwork?

When an agency, packer or distributor orders labels for another brand, identify who can release the brand artwork for that job. Connect the approved file revision to the permitted use and the actual order. Give the converter one clearly released version after the brand's responsible contact has resolved any rights or approval questions.

Identify who controls the supplied assets

A packaging file can combine a brand logo, an illustration, a licensed photograph and type supplied by different parties. Ask the brand contact to identify the source and approval owner for the assets being sent to print. If the job includes licensed material, have that owner check the permitted use against the proposed packaging and order.

The US Copyright Office's permission guidance explains how to identify a copyright owner and request a license when permission is needed. The USPTO's trademark scope overview describes the connection between a mark and the relevant goods or services. These US references help frame the questions. The brand and its advisers resolve the rights applicable to the actual assets, products and markets.

For the production handoff, have one named brand contact release the artwork revision. If an agency or purchasing company sends the file, state the role under which it places that order and who receives the final proof.

Keep the permitted use with the order reference

Use a compact asset record that can travel with the artwork. Keep commercially sensitive contracts with the responsible owner and give the converter the relevant production instructions.

Field What to connect
Asset reference The exact logo or artwork version used in the label file
Rights or brand contact The party responsible for confirming the proposed use
Proposed use Product, packaging format and market, with relevant quantity or time limits where the applicable agreement contains them
Allowed adaptations Changes such as small-size simplification, color treatment or combining the mark with another graphic
Order release The final label revision, job or purchase-order reference and approving contact

These are suggested handoff fields. The required permissions and evidence depend on the asset and agreement. If a permission question remains unresolved, the responsible owner should resolve it before the affected file goes into production.

Return artwork changes to the right owner

A printer may propose thickening a fine line or simplifying a small mark to keep it legible. Ask the brand contact to approve the actual adaptation. Keep the original asset and the production version identifiable, so a later reorder can use the approved adaptation without quietly changing it again.

The small-size hologram logo guide addresses that visual review. For holographic work, also state whether the logo is a surface imprint or part of a proposed custom optical image. Those require different production instructions and samples.

Consider two fictional orders that use the same logo asset. Order A names a released label revision and a brand approver. Order B contains an agency draft that still awaits the brand's revision. Copying A's logo file into B can help prepare B's layout. B's contact still needs to release the completed artwork for B's product and order.

Certification and program marks have their own owners and rules. Keep the applicable program's label approval with the relevant artwork when one is used. The organic-seal review is one product-specific example.

Send one released production file

Prepare an RP Labels inquiry with the package, label size, quantity per design and required print or holographic treatment. Identify the party ordering, the brand contact and the released artwork revision. Supply relevant brand-use instructions with the proof request, including which changes need another approval.

For a custom holographic label, ask the quote to identify the physical customization and sample scope. Keep the brand's artwork release connected to that proposal. A new product, market or adapted mark can then return to its responsible owner before the next print order.
