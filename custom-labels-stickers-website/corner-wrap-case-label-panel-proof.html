Corner-Wrap Case Labels: Proof Both Panels Before Ordering

Suppose a supplement co-packer ships a filled corrugated case that can reach receiving with either of two adjacent faces toward the operator. The case data has already been approved. The buyer needs to settle whether one label can wrap the corner or whether the line should apply two separate labels.

Specify a one-piece wrap if the intended applicator can place it consistently and every required symbol, including its quiet zones, fits on its assigned flat face across normal placement variation. Use two separate labels if the fold or application variation compromises that fit and the line can place and reconcile both labels. If either layout leaves a required view unreadable, resolve the face map with the receiver before ordering.

Mark the faces receiving can actually see

Set a filled, closed case on its natural base. Mark the two candidate faces and the working corner. Have the receiver confirm which face or faces must show which approved information, whether the same symbol may or must appear on both, and what the scanner can see in the normal arrival position. Check whether neighboring cases, stretch film or handling orientation obstruct those views. For the upstream data decision, use the case identity and lot proof guide.

For GS1 general-distribution applications, the General Specifications address vertical-edge clearance for carton and outer-case symbols, including their quiet zones. They recommend a second side for GTIN-identified outer cases or cartons when the print process makes duplication cost-effective. The same section gives separate guidance for SSCC-identified pallets. Use the clauses that apply to the case and confirm the trading partner’s requirements. GS1 General Specifications, release 26.0, sections 6.7.1.2 and 6.7.2

Compare the wrap against two flat labels

One corner-wrap label. Mark a fold band wide enough to cover the observed or agreed range where the corner can land. Keep symbols and their required clear space entirely outside that band. Measure usable space on both faces after allowing for edge clearance, seams or flaps. Have the packer check the intended print-and-apply equipment, label feed and case orientation. Videojet describes equipment designed to place a single label across adjacent case faces. The packer confirms the fit for its own equipment and case. Videojet corner-wrap applicator overview

Two separate flat labels. Give each label a position and full symbol area on its own face. Check whether two applicators or two passes are practical. For a design that repeats data, define how the line keeps the labels tied to the same approved case record after a restart, reprint or rejected label. Assign the case hold and reconciliation decision if one label is missing or the fields disagree.

Test the filled case and assign the checks

  1. Packer’s line owner. Apply trial labels to representative packed cases. Include the permitted orientations and normal case and placement variation. Mark where the corner lands within the proposed fold band. Check for wrinkles, bridging and lift, especially where a defect could cross into a symbol or its clear space.
  2. Receiving lead. At each required approach, find the intended label, read its text and scan the required symbols on the ordinary equipment. Compare what decodes with the approved case record. Document any orientation that obscures a required field and agree which layout or handling change resolves it.
  3. Named verification owner. If the project requires a formal barcode quality grade, agree the applicable method, access to each symbol and acceptance criteria before release. Keep the verifier’s report with the case proof and record the operational scan separately.

Keep the final face map, format, applicator, case reference and receiver sign-off with the order. A different case size, material, seam or applicator can change the usable area. Use the map to identify which parts of the trial to repeat.

Request pricing for the case layout

For a supplement packaging label project, send the proposed case-label scope to RP Labels. Include the packed case or drawing, face material and dimensions, working edge, required views, approved symbol types and data source, applicator details, case variants and quantity by design. A redacted face map can show the available space. Ask which supplied formats and samples can be quoted, and identify the teams that will carry out receiving and verification checks.
