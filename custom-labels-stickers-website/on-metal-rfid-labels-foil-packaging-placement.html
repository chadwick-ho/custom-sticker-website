On-Metal RFID Labels for Foil Packaging: Test the Placement Before Ordering

If a retail partner requires item-level UHF RFID on a foil-containing pack, start by checking whether the packaging and proposed tag locations are allowed. If they are, compare a permitted location genuinely offset from the foil with a specialty on-metal construction approved by the partner for the application. Make the decision on the filled saleable unit and in its real case or display arrangement, not on an empty pouch or a loose inlay.

Rule out prohibited constructions before you spend on a pilot

Record the partner program and version, what counts as one saleable item, the allowed tag format and locations, and any inlay or approval rules. Ask whether foil is permitted in the RFID-bearing packaging at all, including decorative layers, and whether a specialty tag needs a separate submission. The Auburn RFID Lab ALEC placement guide flags the effects of foil, metal and liquids and advises against metal foil, holograms, and metallic inks on packaging that contains an RFID inlay. It also gives guidance by packaging type. The GS1 US EPC placement guideline covers apparel and general merchandise; it does not grant permission for every foil pack. If the applicable program bars the construction or placement, stop and seek an approved redesign or written program direction. A read demonstration is not a waiver.

Draw two placement candidates, if both are eligible

Ask the packaging converter for the actual layer map. Mark where the foil ends and what sits behind a proposed tag after the pack is filled and closed. Include foil seals, folds and seams, nearby product or liquid, the position of adjacent units in a case, and the existing printed barcode and its quiet zone. A non-metal-looking outer print area is not necessarily free of foil underneath.

  • Offset from foil. Consider a partner-permitted non-metal region or separate approved carrier only if the tag remains offset in the finished pack and normal handling. Mark how it is located and retained. The flat dieline may show a separation that disappears when the pouch is folded or when units are packed face to face.
  • Specialty on-metal construction. If the program allows this alternative, request the exact candidate construction and approved placement from the RFID provider. Specify the substrate and geometry it will sit on. Avery Dennison's on-metal UHF example shows that this category exists; a generic “on-metal” description is not evidence that a particular design fits this pack or is accepted by the partner.

Check the filled pack, then the real case orientations

Build pilots with the intended fill, seals, artwork and placement. Record each candidate's exact tag construction, location, sample ID, and expected Electronic Product Code (EPC) independently of the reader output. Use the settings and acceptance criteria agreed with the retail partner or its validation provider. Compare individual units and the actual packed orientations, including the arrangement and neighboring items expected at the relevant read points. For each configuration, record missed expected IDs, unexpected IDs, and the orientation and read setup. If results depend on a particular arrangement, document that limit and have the partner decide whether it is acceptable.

Inspect the printed barcode separately on the assembled package. Keep the full symbol and its required clear margin free of tag edges, artwork, folds, and other obstructions. A radio read does not confirm optical readability; a barcode scan does not confirm that the intended EPC was read. The multipack window barcode guide is useful for that separate optical layout review; the RFID and barcode workflow comparison explains why the two records should stay separate. If neither candidate satisfies the applicable requirements, put the order on hold and review packaging or placement with the partner.

For a packaging-print inquiry, send RP Labels the dieline and foil layer map, filled unit and case orientation, partner program and version, marked barcode area, target print stock and finish, and quantity by SKU. Specify the printed scope separately from the inlay, encoding, RF pilot, and approval; confirm those responsibilities with the appropriate providers before ordering.
