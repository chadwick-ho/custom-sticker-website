Before You Order Holographic Labels

Bring these decisions together before comparing designs:

Decision What to define
Visual effect Your logo and reference appearance; whether customization concerns printed artwork or the optical pattern itself
Seal structure Where the label sits, what opening path it crosses and what visible change you want to inspect
Printed data No code, shared content or a requested individual identifier; artwork space and data source
Verification responsibility Who checks the visual reference or scan response, and who maintains any linked records

The decorative versus security guide helps define the feature's purpose. A holographic appearance alone does not demonstrate opening evidence or authenticate the product.

Define What Is Custom in the Visual Effect

A logo printed over an existing holographic pattern and a proposed custom optical pattern are different scopes. Describe what should remain visible as the label is tilted, and identify which parts of your reference are printed color, logo detail or optical movement.

Use the stock versus custom guide to prepare the request. Ask which route can be quoted for your project and which reference or sample would demonstrate it. Where optical origination is proposed, agree the master scope and associated terms before approval. A flat artwork proof reviews layout; it does not reproduce an optical effect in motion.

Specify the Closure and Opening Evidence

For a bottle, show the cap, neck and body surfaces that the seal would contact. For a carton, show the flap, opening edge and board coating. Include dimensions, texture, curvature and how the package is opened. A body decoration or lid-top label may have a different placement purpose from a seal crossing an opening.

If you need a visible change on removal, describe the evidence you want to inspect. The VOID versus destructible guide compares message-reveal and fragmentation concepts. Ask which constructions are available for the proposed surface, then agree applied-sample conditions and acceptance observations. Confirm where any message appears and how removal affects both label and package; the construction name alone is not the approval result.

Set the Data and Scan Scope

State whether the artwork needs a shared QR destination, a printed serial field or a requested unique identifier for each item. Include example content and explain what a scan should show. The static versus unique QR guide helps separate shared content from item-level identity.

Before data printing is included, agree its availability, file format, generation and checking responsibilities, and how label data will be matched to your records. Identify who owns the destination, database and response to an unexpected or repeated scan. Printed identifiers and a verification service are separate deliverables; a unique code alone does not make an item uncopyable.

Prepare Artwork, Quantities and Application Details

Send the logo or artwork, package photographs and dimensions, requested label shape, pieces per design and application method. Mark the areas reserved for text, codes and optical features. Discuss contrast and placement on the proposed material, with physical code-readability checks where relevant.

If a machine will apply the labels, include the required unwind view, core size, maximum roll diameter and spacing. For hand application, state your preferred format. Confirm available supply formats with the proposed construction, alongside the design assistance, digital proof and physical sample scope and charges.

Compare First Orders and Reorders

Ask for a quote that separates label production from any proposed origination, tooling, data preparation or sampling. For an unchanged reorder, confirm what is retained, what can be reused and what is charged again. Changes to the design, construction or data may change the scope.

The hologram cost and MOQ guide helps organize the comparison. Quantity minimums, timing, master retention and reuse terms belong in the written project offer.
