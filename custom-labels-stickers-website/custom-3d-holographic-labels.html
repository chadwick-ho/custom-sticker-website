Buying decisions

Before you order holographic labels

A shiny sticker, a custom logo and a seal that shows opening are different specifications. Choose the job first so that two quotes describe the same result.

Stock pattern or custom logo hologram?

A stock design can suit a project that accepts the existing appearance. For custom work, ask whether your logo is printed over a stock optical pattern or forms part of an originated optical design. Compare the offered sample and setup scope, not just the word “custom.”

Compare stock, imprint and optical scope
Decorative finish or a security check?

A reflective finish can meet a visual branding brief. If someone must inspect evidence of opening or a controlled visual feature, specify what that person checks and what an accepted sample looks like. Shine alone does not establish that function.

Give the feature a defined job
Shared QR content or individual identity?

A shared URL can be enough for product information. A unique code needs controlled data and an owned destination; authentication also needs an operated checking process. Printing a code does not create that service.

Decide what each scan must know

Quote the package and the intended check

Send the package surface and photo, label size and position, artwork, quantity and required visual effect. For a seal, describe the opening motion and the evidence you want left behind; compare VOID and destructible constructions on the intended package. For QR or serial work, identify the data owner and destination, then plan an applied-code scan check.
