How to Proof Per-Unit and Per-Serving Supplement Facts Columns

When a capsule or gummy brand asks for a per-unit column, start with the approved per-serving panel and the directions for that exact SKU. Keep the per-serving declaration as the primary basis. Add an optional column only after the brand's regulatory reviewer approves the basis, display values and presentation. Hold the proof if the directions, the serving unit or any column entry differs from the approved files.

Match the directions, serving declaration and fill version

Request the approved directions elsewhere on the label, the approved Supplement Facts copy and the finished dieline. Record the revision of each and confirm they cover the same SKU and fill count. Check the unit term and the approved number of units per serving, then compare the servings-per-container declaration where applicable. For the columns, request the final display entries, units, applicable percentage Daily Values and footnotes from the party that owns the data. The per-serving declaration follows 21 CFR 101.36(b). Current 21 CFR 101.36(b)

Here is a paper-proof scenario. The signed files identify a two-capsule serving, and the reviewer has separately approved values for one capsule. The team should be able to point from each item on the proof back to the approved source:

  • Serving declaration. Match the displayed unit and count to the approved serving declaration and check their relationship to the approved directions.
  • Per-serving column. Match each entry to the approved per-serving values, including units, symbols and associated notes.
  • Per-unit column. Match the approved heading and each entry to the signed one-capsule data for the same SKU.

If the designer has only the per-serving file, leave the optional proof on hold and request the missing display values. This fictional paper proof illustrates an artwork handoff. Use the reviewer's exact display values when preparing the real proof.

Check the basis and position of the additional information

Under § 101.36(e)(9)(i), per-unit information may supplement the required per-serving information. Place it in clearly identified additional columns to the right. A reviewer looking at the proof should be able to follow any row to the correct heading. Current 21 CFR 101.36(e)(9)(i)

The per-day alternative in § 101.36(e)(9)(ii) applies when another part of the label recommends consuming the supplement more than once per day. A serving with several units can still have one recommended consumption occasion. Have the reviewer point to the qualifying directions and approve the proposed day basis and presentation. If using a per-day column, check that it sits to the right of the per-serving information with a clear heading and the prescribed parenthetical reference to the daily recommendation. The rule also describes a parenthetical format in the serving-size declaration, with specific wording limits. Have the reviewer check that format directly if the artwork uses it. Current 21 CFR 101.36(e)(9)(ii)

Do not turn the serving-unit count into a daily frequency

Two units in one serving do not establish that the product is recommended twice a day. Keep the approved units-per-serving field separate from the frequency stated in the directions. The fictional records below deliberately use the same serving-unit count with different occasion counts to show why the artwork team must read both inputs.

Use the actual reviewer-approved display values for the requested column. Do not calculate a per-unit column by halving rounded per-serving entries, or infer a per-day presentation from the number of capsules in one serving. If the source does not identify the intended basis, return that question to the copy and regulatory owner. Include the approved panel and directions revisions in the RP Labels inquiry.

Compare two proofs at actual size

Print the approved per-serving baseline and the proposed alternate at finished size on the same dieline. Wrap both around the intended bottle. Follow one row from its name to each corresponding value. Check every heading, alignment, unit, rule, symbol and footnote. Inspect the seam, overlap and curve for any hidden or misleadingly aligned text. If the added column crowds the required copy, return to the reviewer with the two marked proofs and the measured space. The Supplement Facts dieline guide covers how to document that fit issue.

Stop artwork release if an entry cannot be traced to its approved source, the directions are newer than the panel, or the column layout changes the meaning of an entry. Send the marked discrepancy and exact revision to the responsible data or regulatory reviewer. Use the corrected approved files to proof the affected SKU again.

Quote the label against the approved copy

For custom supplement labels, prepare the actual bottle or drawing, final dieline, approved panel and directions by SKU, and the alternate column or wording the reviewer approved. Include the marked proof, ink and finish, quantity per version, and the named person who signs off the final presentation. Ask for the print and proof scope on that revision. Discuss the Supplement Facts artwork with RP Labels.
