Custom Keg Collars for Rotating Draft Beer

Order a beer-specific keg collar when the beer name and approved copy will stay in use long enough to consume the print run. For short or changing fills, a common collar can reduce leftover versions if the crew can enter the right beer at filling and someone can still read and verify it at dispatch. A brewery with a steady house lager and rotating seasonals can reasonably buy both formats.

Count what each format leaves the crew to do

Start with the filling schedule for the period the artwork will remain valid. For each beer, write down expected fills, usable collars already on hand and how many more a dedicated run would put into storage. Flag a seasonal whose next brew date is uncertain. A cheap unit price has little value if half the collars still name last season’s beer.

For a fair cost comparison, ask for quantities and setup charges by version. Add the brewery’s own time to pick, complete and check collars. Include leftover dedicated stock in the estimate. A common design works only for beers that can share its fixed copy under the brewery’s marking plan. Compare print quotes on the same delivered scope.

Map the collar, the permanent marks and the warning

Give the brewery’s label reviewer a top and side photo of the actual keg. Mark the permanent brewer identification, the production-location marking, the approved health-warning location and the information that changes with the fill. Have the reviewer confirm each component for the keg size, beer and destination before asking a printer to reserve space for it.

For U.S. malt beverages, 27 CFR 7.51(b) addresses qualifying collars or tap covers carrying specified mandatory information on kegs of at least 5.16 gallons. One route treats the collar as firmly affixed when removal destroys it for reuse. Another permits a removable collar when the applicable bottler or importer name is marked on the keg in a manner specified in the rule. Check the conditions against the actual attachment and keg marking. 27 CFR 7.51.

The government health warning has its own placement and attachment requirements under Part 16. Section 25.141 also addresses permanent brewer identification and how the place of production can be shown. Have the reviewer locate the warning on the keg and verify it remains visible with the collar fitted. Record the brewery’s approved arrangement, including any applicable state requirements. TTB keg labeling guidance, page 43 · Title 27, sections 16.21, 16.22 and 25.141.

Fit one of each design on the keg

Check the center opening, outer edge and proposed slit or attachment with the filling operator. Ask them to fit the sample as they would during a changeover and confirm access to the fitting and handles. For the common collar, have them enter the longest beer name with the designated pen or printer. Check for clipped letters, slow-drying ink and a field that disappears under the keg rim. A writable coating is one commercially described option to evaluate with your marking method. InTouch keg collar constructions.

After normal cold storage and wet handling, give the keg to the person who would pick it for dispatch. Ask them to identify the beer and compare their reading with the fill record. Record the keg model, collar revision, marking tool and any ambiguous entry. If two seasonals are easily confused, revise the field or move those beers to dedicated prints before releasing the order.

Request a quote by beer and keg format

Send the keg capacity, top and side photos, approved component map, neck dimensions, attachment preference and beer list. Give quantities for each dedicated version and a separate total for any common stock. Include how the crew completes variable fields and the cold or wet conditions the finished collar will encounter.
