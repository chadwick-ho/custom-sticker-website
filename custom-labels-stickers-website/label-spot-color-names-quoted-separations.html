Spot Color Names on Labels: Match the Quoted Separations

Before approving a label quote based on its color count, ask the designer and converter to reconcile named artwork separations with the proposed output. Two blue-looking objects can have different spot names. White-ink and cutting instructions can appear in the list for different reasons.

The buyer needs to know what the quote will produce and charge for. Do not count colors by eye, assume every named entry is a physical ink, or merge everything that looks similar on a screen.

Compare names, roles and proposed output

One fictional blue element uses BrandBlue, another Brand Blue. Similar names and schematic screen colors do not establish whether these mean one intended ink, two deliberate colors or differently defined objects. Ask prepress to identify their affected areas and proposed output.

List the actual separation name, purpose, location, proposed treatment and related quote line. Distinguish process-color artwork, intended spot ink, opaque white, foil or finishing instructions and cut paths. Their presence in a file does not give them identical production roles.

For a process-only proposal, ask how named spots will be handled and appearance proofed. For a spot-ink proposal, identify which names correspond to the agreed inks. Availability, production route and charges require project confirmation; this guide declares no standing RP spot-ink process or pricing formula.

Resolve duplicate names with the converter

Adobe's Ink Manager guidance describes mapping duplicate spot names through an alias so they output to one separation. That is a tool for an established intention, not evidence that similar-looking colors should always be combined.

Have the artwork owner confirm whether the objects should share an ink. Have the converter identify the mapping in its actual workflow. Retain the revised file or identified output instruction and review the resulting separations and proof.

Finding Before release
Two names mean one confirmed intended ink Agree the correction or alias and identify affected output
Similar colors are intentionally distinct Preserve the distinction and confirm quoted scope
Entry means white, foil, varnish or cutting information Confirm its separate role; do not absorb it into brand-color mapping
Quote does not identify the production route Ask what its color count means before comparing prices

Do not apply blanket spot-to-process conversion or rename special entries merely to shorten the list. That can alter a white backing, finish or technical instruction. Ask for a specific mapping and review its effects with the responsible parties.

Return to the complete label

Adobe's separations-preview guidance describes inspecting individual separations. Request the relevant output view with its file revision, then compare the whole proof. A clean name list is not a physical color match.

Check affected logos, small text, code contrast and white or reflective areas. Use the overprint guide for overlap behavior and physical color-master guide for appearance. Confirm whether the change affects setup, tooling or samples instead of assuming a saving.

For custom supplement labels, prepare the inquiry with the submitted revision, dimensions, quantities and uncertain separation names. Ask RP to clarify the proposed printing and finishing scope. A duplicate-name correction does not authorize changing the brand color, construction or accepted price.
