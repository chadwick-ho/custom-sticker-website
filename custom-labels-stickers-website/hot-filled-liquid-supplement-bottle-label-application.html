Hot-Filled Liquid Supplement Bottles: Approve the Labeling Point

Before ordering labels for a hot-filled liquid supplement bottle, ask the co-packer to identify the exact point at which the label meets the container. A bottle labeled after it has cooled and dried needs approval at that condition. A label applied while the bottle is still warm, or before later heating or cooling, needs evidence for the complete sequence it will see.

Find the application point on the actual line

Record filling, cooling, drying, label application and packing in order. Ask the line owner for the measured container surface condition at application and identify any later exposure that occurs after the label is on. The fill temperature alone does not describe what touches the adhesive. Include the bottle resin and finish, the actual panel shape, and any visible change to the container as it cools.

Avery Dennison's adhesive technical guide distinguishes between temperature at application and in later service. An Avery Dennison case study about hot-fill PET packaging also describes packaging designed to respond to the cooling phase. It concerns a particular beverage bottle and labeling route. Ask the bottle supplier whether your supplement container has any comparable movement near the proposed label area.

Approve the route that matches production

For application after cooling, examine the actual surface just before the label is applied, then inspect the finished bottle after planned handling. Do the routing and surface preparation allow the packer to supply a consistent bottle condition? If the label is applied while the pack remains warm, ask the converter to confirm the proposed construction for that measured condition and its later exposure. Have the packer run the agreed route with representative containers before release.

Packing route Evidence to request before ordering
Label after cooling and drying A finished sample applied at the planned station, with the surface condition and container shape recorded.
Apply while the bottle remains warm or before another exposure A representative label construction and a packer trial through the full post-application sequence, including any cooling or contact that matters.
Compare an earlier station with a later station Ask the packer to confirm that each option fits the approved packaging process. Compare equivalent bottle and label references so the application point is the main change.

Inspect the same corners, seam and information fields before and after the relevant stages. A crease after the pack changes shape calls for geometry review. An unbonded edge already present at the labeler calls for contact and application review. Record both separately if they occur together. Hold approval if the sample used an empty room-temperature bottle while the actual line presents a warm filled bottle, or if the later exposure was omitted. The bottle application guide can help record the first observable change.

Send the line conditions with the artwork

Prepare your RP Labels inquiry with the actual bottle reference, label dimensions, artwork, quantities per design and application method. Include the packer's measured application conditions and what the label encounters afterward. Ask which complete label construction and representative sample can be quoted. The packer retains responsibility for the approved filling and cooling process.
