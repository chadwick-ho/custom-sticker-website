Obsolete Branded Labels: Confirm Secure Disposal

When branded label stock has been authorized for retirement, agree its identity, custody and final-disposition evidence before arranging external collection. A pickup receipt can show that stock left a site without showing that the agreed disposal was completed. Keep the job open until the final record can be connected to the authorized material, with any exceptions resolved.

This guide concerns unused printed labels held at a brand, converter or packer. It starts after the owner has decided which stock may leave use. The task is to purchase and close out the external disposal service for that stock, including ordinary branded labels without individual serial numbers.

Define the stock authorized to leave use

Have the brand's responsible owner identify the artwork versions, SKUs and locations covered by the disposal authorization. Identify who has authority over stock held at another company. An instruction to “clear old labels” is too broad if some material remains authorized for use or must be retained as a reference.

Resolve retained-reference exceptions before the collection list is issued. Record the sample identity, amount, location, custodian and reason for retaining it. Keep retained material outside the disposal quantity and distinguish it from usable production stock. Follow the owner's applicable retention requirements; there is no universal retention period supplied by this guide.

If the decision about using old stock is still open, resolve it through the artwork-changeover process first. A disposal booking should not become an accidental authorization to retire stock that was only awaiting review.

Give each collection a traceable stock list

Create a collection reference that connects the authorization to the physical rolls or cartons. Include the site, artwork revision, roll or carton IDs, material and liner description, quantity basis and the person responsible for the handoff. Describe partial rolls and mixed cartons explicitly.

Use counts or weights in the form actually available, and distinguish measured values from estimates. A carton count at pickup and a weight at treatment are not directly comparable without an agreed connection. Do not convert weight into label pieces using an invented average.

Record field What the buyer needs to establish
Authorized stock Owner's approval, SKU or artwork revision, locations and affected stock IDs
Collection units Roll/carton/container identities and the list they refer to
Quantity basis Pieces, rolls, cartons or weight; actual measurement or stated estimate
Retained references Identified exceptions, custodian and separate amount
Custody transfers Who releases and receives the identified units, where and when
Final disposition Provider's completion record tied to the collection and agreed treatment
Open difference Missing, rejected or unmatched material, with an owner and next action

For labels with individual identifiers, use the separate serialized-label reconciliation guide for the code records. Here, the collection reference still needs to follow the physical material through the external service.

Confirm material acceptance and custody before booking

Give the provider the actual material description: paper or film labels, adhesive, liner, rolls or sheets, and any special decorative construction. Ask whether its proposed service accepts that combination and packing form. An offer for ordinary office-paper shredding does not establish acceptance of adhesive label rolls.

Agree where collection takes place, who controls the material during transport and temporary storage, and whether another facility or subcontractor will handle it. Ask who remains responsible for reporting across those handoffs. If security seals or other container controls form part of the agreement, record their identifiers and the procedure for discrepancies.

Define the intended outcome in terms the provider can accept and document, such as rendering the identified branded labels unusable for their original labeling purpose. Agree the treatment method, exceptions, completion evidence and charges with that provider. Request a sample record format before collection so both parties understand what the final documentation will establish.

Read the document's scope, not just its title

Shred-it's US specialty-service page describes a Proof of Service Certificate issued after pickup to acknowledge collection for secure destruction. That wording establishes the collection stage; it should not be treated as a statement that final destruction has already occurred. Shred-it specialty services.

As a separate service example, Canadian provider Blue-Pencil describes a completion certificate with details such as the material quantity or weight, treatment method, location, date and personnel. These are useful fields to discuss with a prospective provider. They are that company's stated service terms, not a US legal requirement or an industry-wide guarantee. Blue-Pencil product-destruction service.

At pickup, match the accepted collection units to the authorized list and record differences immediately. At closeout, check whether the final document identifies the same collection, states the agreed completed action and addresses the material actually received. The title “certificate” cannot repair a missing connection between that document and your stock.

Consider a hypothetical handoff: the collection record identifies cartons A and B, but the later completion record names only A. Keep B open and ask the provider to explain its status and supply the missing evidence. Do not infer that B was treated because both cartons left on the same vehicle. This is a planning example, not a record of a completed disposal job.

Close exceptions before calling the job complete

The responsible owner can close the disposal record when the final evidence satisfies the agreed scope, the collection is matched, and retained references and discrepancies have their own documented status. If the provider rejects a material or reports a different quantity basis, preserve the original record and resolve the difference with the custodian and provider.

Recycling is a separate outcome to verify. Do not assume every mixture of label, adhesive, liner and decorative material becomes recyclable after destruction. The release-liner collection guide concerns a separately accepted liner stream; it is not acceptance for intact obsolete branded-label rolls.

Keep the replacement-label order separate

If retiring this stock also requires replacement supplement labels, prepare the new print request using the released artwork, actual package, quantity by design, application requirements and delivery destination. Refer to custom supplement labels for that purchasing scope.

Prepare the replacement-label inquiry and ask RP Labels to confirm the proposed construction, proof or sample scope and charges. Keep the disposal provider's collection and completion obligations in its own agreement. If a supplier holds old stock, first confirm its inventory and contractual responsibilities; do not send obsolete material to a label supplier without a separately accepted arrangement.
