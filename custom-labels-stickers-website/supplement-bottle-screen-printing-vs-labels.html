Screen Printing vs Labels for Supplement Bottles

For an already selected supplement bottle, compare direct screen printing and a separate adhesive label on that same bottle, using the same approved copy. Choose the route that meets the finished-decoration requirements and leaves an acceptable amount of stock exposed to the next artwork change. Neither route has a universal price or durability advantage.

Direct printing may be a suitable choice when its appearance, sample results and commitment of decorated bottles fit the project. Pressure-sensitive labels may be useful when the brand wants to hold undecorated bottles for several designs and assign the printed identity later. That flexibility depends on when labels are applied: a warehouse of already labeled bottles still carries the old artwork.

Start with one bottle and two defined constructions

Identify the bottle supplier's reference, material, color, drawing revision and surface condition. Give both prospective suppliers the actual bottle and artwork at intended size. A screen-printed sample on a different resin or a label shown on a different bottle color cannot settle this comparison.

In screen printing, ink passes through a screen onto the container surface. A pressure-sensitive label is a separate construction with an adhesive side that is applied to the container. These are the basic process distinctions described in O.Berk's bottle-decorating overview. Its service terms and performance descriptions do not establish the outcome for another supplier's bottle.

Ask the direct printer to identify the proposed ink system, any surface preparation, and the processing conditions that must be complete before reviewing samples. Ask the label supplier to identify face material, adhesive, print and protective finish, with the application owner confirming how the label reaches the bottle. Those details define what each sample represents.

Compare difficult artwork areas on the physical bottle

Mark the areas that could change the decision: the smallest approved copy, adjacent colors, a solid background, a fine logo detail and the later coding area. Have each supplier identify any artwork adjustments its route requires before producing the comparison sample.

Review the same information at the same intended viewing size. Check whether the bottle color shows through, whether small characters remain distinguishable, and whether the graphic sits in the agreed location. A flat proof can settle content and positioning instructions; it cannot establish the appearance of ink on a curved bottle or of the selected label after application.

Keep visual approval separate from handling performance. Describe the project's actual packing, transport and use conditions, then agree appropriate sample checks with the responsible suppliers. Record the sample identity, preparation or conditioning specified by that supplier, method and observations. Do not borrow a curing interval or a durability claim from an unrelated decorating page.

For the label route, include edges and the agreed seam or gap in the applied review. For direct print, inspect the decorated area and any contact points relevant to handling. Both routes also need a separate answer for later lot coding if it is part of the project. An attractive fixed graphic does not approve that later operation.

Map what the next copy change would strand

Choose one plausible revision, such as a brand-approved change to fixed text. List stock by its physical state before estimating replacement cost. This is a planning exercise; the product owner must decide whether any old artwork can still be used.

Stock at the time of revision Direct screen-print route Separate-label route
Undecorated bottles No old printed identity yet; confirm suitability for the revised job No old printed identity yet; confirm suitability for the revised label
Printed components awaiting packing Already decorated bottles carry the old copy Unapplied label stock carries the old copy; plain bottles may remain separate
Bottles already labeled or decorated Review the identified bottle inventory Review the identified bottle inventory, as well as remaining loose labels
Proposed rework Ask the decorator whether any route is feasible and acceptable Ask the application and quality owners whether removal and relabeling are feasible and acceptable

Do not assume a permanent adhesive label can be removed without residue or damage. Equally, do not assume that direct printing can be corrected economically. Until a rework route has been accepted, treat affected stock as an unresolved inventory decision.

The useful distinction is the point at which the brand commits a bottle to an artwork version. If both routes decorate the entire order immediately, separate label production alone does not preserve much bottle flexibility. If accepted plain bottles can remain unassigned until a later packing run, record the quantity and location of that stock separately.

Price the accepted route and its revision exposure

Request quantities by design and identify the costs of setup, tooling, decoration, application where relevant, packing and delivery. Include who owns bottle losses during the proposed operation and whether any unused components remain at a supplier. Use actual quotes rather than a generic volume at which one process supposedly becomes cheaper.

The printed-labels versus labeled-bottles guide helps resolve who supplies each delivered item. Once those responsibilities are clear, use the delivered-cost comparison for the commercial totals. Here, the additional decision is whether the chosen decoration and its inventory consequences suit this bottle program.

Release a route when the identified finished sample is accepted, the operating responsibilities are assigned, and the first-order and change assumptions are documented. Hold the choice if the comparison still uses different bottles, an unidentified label construction or a sample that excludes a decisive artwork area. A lower quoted unit price does not close those gaps.

Prepare the adhesive-label side of the inquiry

For custom supplement labels, provide the bottle reference and material, dimensioned artwork and revision, quantity per SKU, desired appearance, later coding area and application owner. Add the handling conditions and the copy-change scenario that matter to the comparison.

Prepare a label inquiry for RP Labels and ask which adhesive-label construction and sample scope can be quoted for those inputs, including charges and delivery destination. Obtain the direct screen-print proposal from the provider responsible for that operation. Keep bottle supply, direct printing and label application explicitly assigned in their respective offers.
