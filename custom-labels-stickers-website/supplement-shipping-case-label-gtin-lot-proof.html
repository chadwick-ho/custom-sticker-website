Supplement Shipping Case Labels: Match Case Identity and Lot

Before ordering shipping-case labels, establish exactly what each case contains and which approved identifier and lot record belong to it. A bottle SKU, a case configuration and a pallet are different objects. The printed code must match the physical case and the receiving system, not merely look like the bottle barcode.

Map bottle, case and pallet as separate objects

Start with the brand owner's product master: bottle SKU and item GTIN, units per case, case description and approved case GTIN. A case GTIN identifies a trade-item configuration; it does not by itself distinguish individual physical cases. A lot identifies a production group and is separate data. A pallet or other logistic unit may have an SSCC identifying that logistic unit; it is not the case GTIN. See the pallet SSCC printing decision for who assigns and binds that identifier.

In a hypothetical example, a brand packs 12 identical bottles of one SKU per case. The approved master links that bottle GTIN to the 12-count case GTIN. If the brand changes the case configuration, it must review the identifier and master data under applicable GS1 rules with its GS1 organization. The example contains no usable production number.

Confirm configuration and identifier with the brand owner

The brand owner is responsible for the approved product and identifier data; confirm the actual assignment arrangements with the brand owner and GS1. GS1 US describes creating a case record with the contained product and quantity, and distinguishes a mixed case containing multiple GTINs. Do not derive a case GTIN by changing digits on a bottle code yourself. Obtain the approved case identifier and description from the responsible party, including a separate decision for mixed cases.

Ask the customer or receiving partner which symbology, data fields, placement, print quality and electronic records it requires. GS1 US describes an ITF-14 option for a shipping case; it does not mean every receiver accepts that symbol for every combination of data. The case identifier and lot may require different carriers or a different agreed format. Have the appropriate GS1 and receiver specifications checked for the chosen application.

Decide whether the receiver needs the lot inside the barcode

A case can show a lot number in plain text while its barcode carries only the GTIN. Ask the receiving partner which fields its system needs from the scan. Text beside a symbol is not automatically encoded in it.

GS1 US's carrier guidance identifies ITF-14 as a GTIN-only carrier using a 14-digit representation. Cases with the same case GTIN and different lots can therefore return the same GTIN from that symbol. The fictional cases show why a successful read alone does not establish lot capture.

If the receiver needs only the approved case GTIN, an accepted ITF-14 route may be appropriate. If it needs lot or date in the machine-readable handoff, obtain its accepted carrier and data structure. GS1 US's barcode overview describes GS1-128 and certain 2D options for extra data; it does not establish which one the actual partner accepts.

A different symbol name alone does not add the fields. The data owner supplies authorized values, the barcode owner defines encoding, and the receiver confirms that its equipment and application parse them for this case.

Have the print or packing owner scan the proposed label through the intended receiving workflow. Retain raw decode and parsed case/lot fields, then compare them with the controlled contents record. If lot is absent from machine data, resolve the route before releasing labels instead of silently changing the receiver's process.

For the supplement-label inquiry, attach this decision to the case brief: approved reference, quantity, changing fields, who prints them and the receiver's guide. The proposed supply is printed stock/data reproduction; it does not include identifier allocation or warehouse-system integration unless separately confirmed.

Connect each lot to the correct finished case

Map the lot and any expiry from the authorized production record to the actual contents. Agree who supplies the data, prints and applies the variable field, checks it at the line, and reconciles unused or incorrect labels. The GS1 US traceability guidance discusses linking product identification with production data; the particular trading-partner workflow still needs agreement.

If the case is repacked or contains multiple lots, hold it until the responsible team decides the permitted identification and record. Do not assume one printed lot represents a mixed-lot case. Record corrections and the person who approves release; if pallets are rebuilt, manage the pallet record separately.

Proof the case label at receiving

On a loaded sample case, compare the human-readable description, case GTIN, quantity, lot and any date to the approved master and actual contents. Scan the proposed symbol with the equipment and process used by the packer and receiver, then compare the decoded data with the receiving record. A successful scan alone does not prove correct allocation or formal barcode quality. Agree any independent verification and acceptance criteria with the receiver before release.

For supplement label projects, request a quote with the case surface and dimensions, SKU and pack count, approved identifier and lot data, printer ownership, receiver specifications, quantity and destination. Confirm the printing and verification scope for the specific job.
