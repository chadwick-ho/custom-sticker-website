SKU quote worksheet: how to fill it in

This is a label-printing request for quotation, not a purchase order, supplier acceptance or regulatory approval. The example contains invented planning quantities and dimensions, clearly identified as examples. It contains no prices or accepted minimums.

Use one row per artwork revision. Keep your requested quantity even if the printer proposes a different figure.

Worked use and branches

  1. Replace example bottle dimensions and artwork IDs with confirmed information.
  2. If a proposed material or finish differs, flag it and ask whether that design remains in G1. Never assume that one shared group means mixing delivered rolls.
  3. Send the same request to suppliers and preserve their separate answers. Do not compare totals until every design's scope and quantity are understood.
  4. A hypothetical reply of minimum 200 per design, with no further increment above that, would make the example 200 + 250 + 200 = 650. This is not an RP rule or actual quote. Record it in supplier columns without overwriting the requested 100 + 250 + 150.
  5. If extra stock is useful, compare the complete quote. If a design can wait, confirm the reduced launch. If the requested split is essential, seek an accepted alternative without silently dropping a required property.

Return unresolved quantities or construction changes to the person authorized to approve them. Obtain a project-specific written quotation before placing an order.
