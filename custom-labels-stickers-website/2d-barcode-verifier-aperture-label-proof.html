2D Barcode Verifier Aperture: Can You Compare Two Label Proofs?

Two suppliers send you a grade for the same label artwork. One measured a flat drawdown; the other measured the finished label on the container. Before choosing the higher grade, ask whether both reports use the application-required aperture and lighting, and whether the samples represent the same stage of production. If those conditions differ or are undocumented, do not rank the proofs by grade. Ask for a comparison on a common basis.

Get the application requirement before reviewing either score

Start with where the barcode will be read and who sets acceptance: for example, a retail partner's point-of-sale program or the buyer's internal receiving system. Record the applicable specification and version, symbology, permitted module size (X-dimension), minimum grade, and required measurement settings. If GS1 is specified, identify the relevant Symbol Specification Table in the GS1 General Specifications rather than borrowing settings from another application. The GS1 retail point-of-sale implementation guidance applies to a defined retail use case, not every printed 2D code.

A verifier aperture defines the effective sampling area used to measure the printed symbol. It is not the size of the QR or Data Matrix, a printer's dots per inch, or a setting you can infer from a successful scan. The GS1 2D verification reporting guide describes reporting the overall grade with the measuring aperture and illumination, with the angle reported as applicable under the method. Confirm what an omitted angle means under the specified method; do not assume an undocumented setup was the required one. Use the reporting guide to read the fields, not as a universal pass setting.

Put the two reports on the same basis

Compare Match or document before ranking the grades
Measurement method Application reference, verification method and edition, aperture (with reference number or units), light wavelength or specified broadband condition, and lighting geometry or angle as applicable. Confirm the verifier and calibration status for each report.
Symbol and ID Artwork revision, intended symbology and encoded test data, measured module size, and which symbol was measured if more than one appears on the pack. Keep differences explicit if the trial deliberately changes a design variable.
Physical proof Sample ID, print run or proof lot, media, ink or ribbon, overprint or finish, and whether the sample is a flat proof or applied to the actual package. Include the complete symbol and quiet zone; document the presentation on a curve, seam, or reflective surface if relevant.
Report detail Overall grade, results by parameter and proof ID, specimen count, and any exception or limitation. Ask whether the report covers one selected sample or the specified sampling plan.

If two labs used different apertures, a higher grade does not isolate a print improvement. Do not average the grades or try to convert them. Have the relevant specimens measured with the required settings and equivalent presentation; if a report cannot be reproduced or traced to its specimen, treat it as a screening result. If the finish itself is under comparison, change that factor deliberately, keep the rest of the comparison controlled, and verify the chosen construction in its final presentation. Matching settings make results more comparable; they do not make an unrepresentative sample suitable for release.

Ask what area the reported measurement represents

Aperture is a measurement input, not an artwork-size instruction. The diagram deliberately keeps the square pattern unchanged while changing the conceptual sampling window. It illustrates why a comparison needs a stated measurement basis; it cannot predict how a real defect or printed module will be graded.

The linked GS1 reporting explanation is release 1.3.21 from 2015. Use it to understand the report fields, then have the verifier select the settings required by the actual application and applicable specification. Do not copy an example setting as a universal rule for today's QR or Data Matrix job.

If a proposed proof was assessed under a different aperture, ask the verifier to identify the relevant specimens and the appropriate comparison. Keep the reports separate until that basis is resolved. Include the report references and acceptance owner in the label inquiry; a higher number alone is not a print-release decision.

Keep the print proof and the workflow check separate

Use the verifier report to assess print quality under the stated conditions. Use the intended reader and workflow to check operational decoding and the expected data or destination. A successful phone or line-side scan is useful evidence about that attempt, not a print quality grade. Likewise, a good grade does not confirm that the payload points to the right item or that the live scanner sends it to the right system. If the two checks disagree, keep both results and investigate the failing one instead of treating the other as a substitute. See the broader supplement barcode proof guide.

To scope a supplement label printing project, send RP Labels the production artwork revision, dieline and label size, symbol type and test payload, intended reading point and application specification, target material and finish, mock container details, quantity, and redacted reports if available. Identify who will do the verification and who owns acceptance; request a print quote separately from any third-party verification.
