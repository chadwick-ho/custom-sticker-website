Custom vs Stock Hologram Labels: What Are You Paying For?

A logo printed on rainbow film is customized printing. A logo built into the changing optical image is custom hologram origination. Both can be useful, but they are not the same product and should not carry the same security promise.

The short answer: choose a stock holographic pattern with custom printing when the goal is visual impact, short-run branding or a low-entry decorative seal. Choose a custom master when the optical image itself must carry the logo or defined inspection features. For stronger brand protection, combine the optical layer with tamper evidence, controlled serial data or verification.

Hologram quotations can look identical in a spreadsheet: size, shape, quantity and a line saying "custom logo." The samples arrive, and one shows the logo printed in black over a generic rainbow pattern. The other shows the logo changing, moving and appearing within the diffractive structure.

The first supplier did not necessarily cheat. The buyer and supplier used the same word for two different products. This is where a cheap quote wins on paper and loses when the label is tilted under light.

What a stock-pattern hologram is

Stock material uses an existing holographic pattern: broken glass, dots, pillars, rainbow prisms or another repeating design. The converter can print a logo, product name, QR code or serial number over it and cut the label to a custom shape.

This can be an effective decorative label. It is fast to understand, often easier to start and gives metallic movement that ordinary white film cannot. The important limitation is that the underlying optical pattern may be available to other buyers.

What changes with a custom master

Custom origination creates a diffractive master for the buyer's design. The logo, text, depth, movement or hidden element becomes part of the optical image. Depending on the process, the design may include 2D/3D layering, dot-matrix movement, microtext, guilloche lines or angle-dependent images.

That requires setup, artwork development and approval. The value is not just brighter rainbow color. It is a repeatable optical identity linked to the brand.

Buying rule: ask whether the logo is printed on the hologram or originated inside the hologram. Request a tilt video and physical sample that show the exact quoted construction.

The cosmetics buyer and the warranty buyer

A cosmetics startup wants a reflective round seal on 500 launch boxes. The seal should carry its logo and make the unboxing look more premium. A stock holographic film with controlled white ink and custom print may solve the job honestly.

An industrial-parts brand wants distributors to recognize a specific moving shield, inspect microtext and compare a serial number with shipment records. A generic prism film with a printed logo is not enough. That buyer needs custom optical design, data control and a tamper construction matched to the carton.

Neither buyer should purchase the other's solution. Security features without an inspection process become expensive decoration; decorative material sold as high security creates false confidence.

Overt, fine and data features

Overt featureA visible color shift, moving logo, depth or angle change that staff and customers can inspect without tools.
Fine featureMicrotext or guilloche detail checked with magnification or a trained inspection method.
Data featureSerial number, barcode or QR code connected to controlled records and defined scan behavior.
Physical featureVOID transfer, destructible film or another construction that shows attempted removal.

A strong project gives each layer a job. More features are not automatically better if nobody knows how to inspect them.

White ink and printed design still matter

On holographic material, unbacked process colors allow the rainbow substrate to influence the artwork. Opaque white ink can block selected areas so a logo, barcode or small text remains controlled. A white-ink map should be part of the artwork approval.

Do not cover the entire hologram with white and then wonder where the effect went. Decide where the optical movement should show, where brand color must stay stable and where scanning needs a quiet high-contrast field.

Setup, MOQ and repeat orders

Stock patterns avoid custom optical origination, so they can be practical for smaller or faster projects. Custom masters add setup and lead time, but repeat orders may use the approved master under agreed control.

Ask who owns the master, how long it is retained, whether another converter can use it, what changes require new origination and how unused security labels are handled. These questions matter more than saving a few cents on the first order.

Do not call one shiny feature complete authentication

No label should be sold as impossible to counterfeit. A custom hologram raises the effort required to imitate the optical behavior. Tamper evidence makes transfer harder. Unique data can reveal invalid or repeated codes. Distribution control reduces opportunities for leakage.

The useful result comes from layers and operating discipline. If the company only wants shelf impact, say so and buy a good decorative label. If it wants authentication, document what a genuine label looks like and what staff should do when the check fails.

Approve motion, not a still image

A PDF can confirm dimensions and printed layers but cannot represent optical movement. Review a video under changing light and, for important projects, a physical sample. Check the label at final size because fine features that look impressive enlarged on a monitor may disappear on a 20 mm seal.

Apply samples to the actual box, cap or bottle. Test removal, residue, fracture and visibility against the package color. A security design only works when the finished package allows people to see and use it.

Hologram RFQ checklist

  • Decorative, tamper-evident or authentication objective
  • Stock optical pattern or custom master requirement
  • Logo, movement, depth, microtext and hidden features
  • Printed CMYK, spot color and white-ink areas
  • Serial, barcode or QR data ownership
  • VOID, destructible or permanent construction
  • Application surface, label size and placement
  • Master ownership, MOQ, lead time and reorder terms

FAQ

What is a stock hologram label?

It uses an existing generic holographic pattern and may add printed branding or serial data on the surface. The optical pattern itself is not unique to the buyer.

What is a custom-master hologram?

Its diffractive design is originated for the buyer and can integrate a logo, movement, depth, microtext or hidden elements into the optical image.

Is a custom hologram impossible to counterfeit?

No. A custom master can raise difficulty and support inspection, especially when combined with tamper evidence, variable data and controlled verification.

Which hologram is better for a small order?

A stock-pattern material with custom print may be more practical for a small decorative order. For security, compare master setup and repeat-order economics rather than MOQ alone.
