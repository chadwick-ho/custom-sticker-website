Shelf-Ready Case Labels: Keep the Opening and Shelf View Clear

A case label can be readable at the warehouse and still cause trouble when store staff open the same case. Before ordering labels, test the position on the actual loaded shipper through its intended opening sequence. The decision is where required information belongs in both the transport and shelf states, while keeping the tear path and display usable.

Mark the tear path on the assembled case

Obtain the current case dieline, perforations, seal and opening instructions from the packaging owner. Assemble and load a representative case, close it as it will be shipped, and mark where the operator starts and completes the tear. Include tape, glue, flaps and any wrap that may affect the motion. A flat dieline alone cannot show how a hand reaches the start point or where a torn panel ends up.

The Action retailer packaging guideline addresses easy opening of secondary packaging and visibility of product fronts on the shelf. This is one retailer's example, not a universal US rule. Obtain the actual retailer's current requirements and acceptance process for the intended customer.

Assign label zones for shipping and shelf states

List the information needed before opening, the information that must remain visible afterward, and its owner. Mark candidate label zones on the closed case, avoiding the perforation, opening tab, seams and areas covered by a hand during opening. Check which panel is removed and which becomes the shelf tray. If information must survive opening, place it on a retained panel or provide another approved means; do not assume a label on the removable lid will remain available.

For example, a hypothetical case opens by tearing away its upper panel. The team places a sample shipping label on a retained side panel outside the tear path, then confirms with the retailer whether that information is needed or permitted on the shelf. The position is a trial choice, not a template for every case.

Open a labeled and loaded sample

Apply representative labels in the intended production location, including normal placement variation. With the packaging owner, agree representative shipping and handling conditions and the number of samples before the test. At the end, ask a person following the actual opening instructions to open each loaded case without pre-cutting or helping the perforation. Record whether the label bridges a tear, obstructs the start, tears the tray, obscures the displayed product or removes needed information with the lid.

Photograph the closed case, the opening action and the finished shelf view. Check legibility and access to the required information in each state. If the result is unacceptable, revise the position or case design and repeat the agreed trial; do not infer performance from a mockup alone.

Release the label position and handling brief

Approve a drawing with panel, orientation, placement tolerance, no-label areas and revision. Assign the case maker, label supplier and packer their respective instructions, and ask the retailer to confirm its requirements.
