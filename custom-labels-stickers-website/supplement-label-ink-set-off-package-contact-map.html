How to Map Ink Set-Off Paths Before Ordering Supplement Labels

Before approving a printed supplement label, trace what its printed face touches before and after application. An exterior label applied to a filled, closed bottle and a label applied to empty components before nesting have different contact sequences. Give your packaging reviewer the actual contact map and use conditions before committing to a construction. Hold the material decision if you find a plausible route to a surface that can touch the contents and the reviewer has yet to resolve it.

Follow the printed face from the press to filling

Set-off is transfer from a printed surface to another surface it touches, for example during winding or stacking. With a lined pressure-sensitive label, the printed face may touch the back of the release liner in the adjacent turn. Confirm that on the actual roll. A directly printed packaging web might put the printed face against a surface that later faces the contents. Check the layer sequence and the contacting side from the supplier's drawing or a sample. EuPIA describes set-off alongside direct contact, transfer through material and gas-phase transfer as distinct routes. EuPIA printing ink GMP, 5th revision, terms and definitions

Use one record for each touchpoint. Identify the printed face, the surface it contacts, what that surface touches next, and whether a contents-contact surface is involved or its status is unknown. Include the material and lot references, time in contact, storage and handling conditions, and the person who can confirm the observation.

  • Printing and converting. Ask the converter to identify ink, finish, face stock, adhesive and liner, plus any printed-face contacts in stacking or winding.
  • Roll storage and application. Confirm which face contacts the print in the roll, when the liner is removed, and what staging or application surfaces touch the print. Have the packer check whether any shared surfaces touch package interiors, rims or closure components elsewhere in the operation.
  • Empty staging and filling. Have the packer walk through trays, stacks and nests. Mark where the outside of one part can touch an inside surface, including what happens when parts are separated for filling. Record which surfaces can later contact the supplement under the intended storage and use.

Compare two routes before treating quotes as equivalent

Filled and closed first. In an illustrative route, a liner-backed label goes on a filled, closed bottle and finished bottles are packed upright. Verify what the print touched while in the roll, during dispensing and after application. Check whether the surfaces touched have another role on the line. Record the closed assembly and its use conditions for the reviewer.

Label first, then stack empty parts. In a second illustrative route, empties are labeled before staging. If a labeled exterior can rest against the inner wall of a neighboring empty part, document the exact pair and check whether that inner wall will touch the contents. If a rim stop keeps those faces apart in the actual stack, document the separation with the applicable component and orientation. Have the packer check a representative stack as actually handled. Record the contact geometry separately from any determination about transfer.

Route appearance and transfer questions to the right owners

Record visible offset, staining or sticking for the converter and quality team. Preserve the two contacting surfaces and trace them to the affected material. A visually clean sample can still leave possible substance transfer unresolved. Give the packaging or regulatory reviewer the identified material stack, the actual contacts and the intended use. Ask what supplier evidence or further assessment would apply to the finished construction and conditions. Sun Chemical discusses assessing potential transfer routes against the actual packaging design, materials and use. · Sun Chemical on packaging migration pathways

Tie the review to the package, closure, label and process revisions. If a part changes or the packer starts nesting before fill, send the revised map back to the reviewer. Use the label material documents guide to identify which documents answer the resulting evidence questions. For visible transfer in a delivered roll, use the receiving inspection guide.

Keep the contact observation separate from the assessment result

A useful observation names the two physical surfaces: for example, printed exterior A touching inner wall B in a hypothetical stack. The packer then confirms whether inner wall B later contacts the contents. That record identifies a route for review; it does not report a substance, quantity transferred or safe-use conclusion.

Send the converter the actual contact geometry and construction references, and give the packaging reviewer the intended contents, contact sequence and storage conditions. The reviewer can identify the supplier evidence or assessment needed for that scope. Do not turn a photograph of touching surfaces into a migration result, or treat the absence of visible ink as closure of the question. Include the map and unresolved evidence request with the exterior-label inquiry.

Include the contact map with the print inquiry

For custom supplement labels, send the package and closure drawings, exterior label position, label and finish references, proposed reel orientation, pack and fill sequence, storage conditions and quantities by SKU. Include the contact map, responsible reviewer and the specific material or documentation question that remains open. Ask which exterior-label construction and sample scope can be quoted for the project. Discuss the outside-bottle label with RP Labels.
