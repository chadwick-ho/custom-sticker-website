Gluten-Free Marks on Supplement Labels: Approve Product and Print Method

Treat a gluten-free text claim and a GFCO certification mark as different approval briefs. The product's qualified reviewer owns the claim and its evidence. A private certification mark also needs the program's product coverage and approved use. Do not add a badge to a supplement label because a facility certificate, ingredient sheet or neighboring product carries one.

For the print order, keep the exact SKU, intended market, approved artwork and method of applying the mark together. RP can discuss the label-printing brief; that does not establish certification or permission to use a program's mark.

Start with what the label actually says

For a U.S. gluten-free claim, have the reviewer assess the actual product and wording against 21 CFR 101.91. This guide does not decide whether a formula or test result meets that rule. A private program's logo introduces an additional controlled-asset and certification question; it is not just a more decorative way of writing the same sentence.

If a brand proposes GFCO's mark, obtain the current product-specific certification and the authorized artwork through the brand's responsible program contact. GFCO's 2026 manual, provisions 79 and 80, ties mark use to listed certified products and advance approval of the use and format. A certificate covering another product, or evidence about only an ingredient, does not answer the exact SKU's mark-use question.

Confirm the actual label and application method

The GFCO Brand Standards and Logo Use Policy linked from its certification page requires a product-label proof before printing. It also addresses material changes to approved labels and restricts applying the mark by rubber stamp, ink-jet or adhesive sticker without prior written consent.

That last point matters to a self-adhesive label project. Describe the actual route to the brand's GFCO contact: for example, a complete printed product label versus a later separate mark sticker. Have the program confirm how the policy applies and what approval or consent the job needs. Do not assume the printer can resolve that distinction, or that either route has automatic permission.

Use the current authorized asset and program instructions for size, clear space, color and permitted presentation. Do not redraw the mark from an image search or invent a similar-looking certification badge to avoid the review. The diagrams here show only neutral placeholders, not a reproduction of the real mark.

Make the approval travel with the correct SKU

Evidence in the job record Match to the actual project Hold when
Product certification or claim decision Product identity, variant, market and current scope Only the facility, an ingredient or another SKU is identified
Authorized mark artwork Supplied asset/version and current use requirements A designer has substituted or recreated the badge
Complete label proof Exact artwork revision and relevant program decision Approval refers to a different layout or only a cropped mark
Application method How the label/mark reaches the retail package and any needed consent The method was changed after the approval

Consider a fictional two-SKU print brief. SKU-A has an identified program-approved proof; SKU-B has only a copied logo in the design file. Keep SKU-B unresolved rather than inheriting SKU-A's approval. This does not demonstrate that either product is actually certified or that the printer may release either job. The authorized brand and program owners make those decisions.

For a reorder, compare the product, approved proof, mark asset and application method with the records the brand supplies. If a relevant feature changed, ask the responsible owner whether new approval is required before the print release. The copy-change guide explains how to connect the resulting decision to the exported artwork.

Send a print brief with its permissions identified

For custom supplement labels, prepare the inquiry with the container/dieline, quantity by SKU, approved copy, authorized mark asset, program proof reference and the actual application method. Identify who owns certification, claim review and any program consent. Request the print and sample scope against those supplied records; this article offers no certification, testing, mark license or approval on RP's behalf.
