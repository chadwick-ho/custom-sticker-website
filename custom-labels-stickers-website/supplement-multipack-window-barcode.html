Keep the Right Barcode Visible on a Supplement Multipack

If a windowed supplement multipack is sold as one item, have the brand confirm the pack's retail identifier and put the pack barcode where checkout staff can find it. Check the assembled pack for any bottle barcode visible through the window or another opening. If a bottle can expose its own code, screen it or limit the bottle movement before approving the print and pack layout.

Identify what the shopper actually buys

Have the brand's master-data owner identify the sellable item on the checkout listing. An individual bottle and a distinct retail bundle normally have different identities. Ask for the approved identifier for each relevant layer and keep that record with the artwork. The GS1 guidance for bundles and multipacks describes when the bundle gets its own GTIN. The GS1 General Specifications address obscuring unit-level barcodes inside a retail multipack to avoid checkout confusion.

A display tray can have a different job. If the store opens it and sells bottles separately, confirm how the store receives the display and which barcode staff scan at checkout. For a one-bottle retail carton, confirm which symbol the retailer expects on the assembled sale unit and check whether the window provides a workable presentation. The GS1 barcode quality checklist flags packaging that interferes with scanner access.

Check the window on a closed pack

Use the same bottles, artwork position, insert and closure proposed for production. Ask the packer to put the bottles at the rotation and shift limits the insert allows. Photograph the closed pack in each position. View the window straight on and at angles a cashier could see during handling. Record which code, if any, is exposed and where the outside pack symbol appears.

What you see on the assembled sample What to change
The bottle barcode is always screened Keep the screening feature in the approved packaging design. Confirm that the outer code reads as the intended pack item.
The bottle barcode appears after a bottle turns or slides Ask the packaging team to revise the insert, window or opaque panel, or reposition the unit barcode if the standalone bottle still meets its own requirements. Recheck the new closed-pack sample.
The pack barcode is hard to present or is partly covered Review its position and protected clear space with the designer. Ask for print verification and a checkout test on the assembled pack.

Check the finished pack barcode's print quality separately from whether a scanner can see a wrong code. A good verification report on the exterior barcode leaves the window question open. Record the intended pack identifier and the result from the retail setup so the team can trace an issue to print, pack geometry or the retail item record.

For two symbols intended to identify one bottle during a checkout transition, use the dual-marking placement guide. The barcode verification guide explains how to document the printed symbol specimen.

Ask for a proof against the approved pack

Prepare an RP Labels inquiry with the bottle and outside label drawings, approved symbols, window and insert drawings, print surfaces and quantities. Identify which label components you want quoted and who will approve the assembled pack. Ask what printed samples can be quoted for the proposed supplement label brief.
