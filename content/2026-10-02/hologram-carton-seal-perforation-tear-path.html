A hologram seal can be vivid on a swatch and strangely quiet on a real folding carton. A customer lifts the tuck flap, the box flexes, and the strip either tears cleanly, peels away intact or splits somewhere no one planned. If visible opening evidence is the purpose, the artwork review must include the box-opening motion.

I would ask for an assembled carton and watch how it opens before choosing the seal shape. The seal has to cross the actual opening line and adhere to surfaces that may have different coatings. A drawn line in a flat dieline is useful, but it is not a substitute for a sealed, opened and inspected carton.

Locate the opening path first

Identify which flap the user pulls, where the flap pivots, and how much of the seal lands on each side. The ideal graphic center is not necessarily the mechanical tear center. A narrow bridge can concentrate stress; an off-center bridge may let one adhesive end lift before the intended tear begins. Place the holographic motif so critical branding remains recognizable while the opening evidence is also obvious.

HERMA describes tamper-evident sealing labels for folding boxes and notes that a security perforation can be included. Its sealing-label overview also lists perforations, pre-cut tearing lines, adhesives and challenging surface shapes among the design choices. Those are possibilities, not proof that one construction will work on every coated carton. Ask the converter how the chosen film, perforation and adhesive behave together.

Compare two failure modes

One trial can fail because the seal is too easy to peel intact from a glossy or dusty box. Another can fail because the strip tears before the box is intentionally opened during normal handling. Both are procurement problems. The first weakens visible evidence; the second creates false alarms and wasted packaging. A sample trial should include unopened handling and deliberate opening, not only a photo of a torn strip.

The physical carton matters. Coated board, uncoated board and corrugated material can produce different adhesive behavior. The fold radius, surface finish, storage condition and application pressure all influence the result. If the carton is changing supplier or coating, repeat the trial. An approved label on the old board does not automatically transfer its performance to the new one.

Perforation is not the same as authentication

A tear line helps indicate opening when a specific construction is applied correctly. It does not, by itself, verify the origin of a product. A holographic visual effect can support branding, while a security program may need additional controls such as controlled artwork, variable identifiers or a brand-owned verification process. Do not describe an ordinary decorative hologram as certified anti-counterfeit protection without evidence and a defined threat model.

The example photo shows a conceptual intact carton beside an opened one. It is not an RP adhesive test or a standard-compliance certificate. The buyer should approve a real sample that reproduces the chosen carton stock, coating, seal film, cut and application method. Inspect not only whether the strip tore, but whether a consumer can see the evidence and whether the box still opens without destroying the contents.

What to specify before ordering rolls

Send an assembled carton, flat dieline, opening direction, surface coating details, target seal footprint, application method and any automatic-labeler specifications. Mark exactly where the tear should start and finish. Ask for a physical sample on the production carton, then document the intact appearance, normal-handling result and opened appearance. If the fold or board changes later, treat the seal as a new test item rather than only a color revision. A convincing hologram is easy to admire; a well-planned opening path is what makes the seal useful.
