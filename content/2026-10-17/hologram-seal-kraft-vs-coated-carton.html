A hologram seal looks strong under studio lighting. I ask to see the carton it will actually close. A smooth coated board and an uncoated kraft carton can ask very different things of the adhesive. Approval based only on the reflected color leaves out the fiber surface, fold pressure and opening path.

The AI-generated RP Secure cartons in the photograph show a lifted edge on the rough kraft concept. That is an illustration of a risk to test, not evidence that any RP label failed or passed on a real carton.

Specify the exact board finish and seam

Send the production carton, not just a rendered dieline. Identify coated, varnished, laminated and uncoated areas, and mark any area with dust, recycled fiber texture or heavy ink coverage. If the seal bridges a tuck flap, record the flap overlap and whether the box bows outward after filling. A label can adhere well on a flat test panel and lift where it is forced over a step.

HERMA's guidance for rough cartons explains why a fibrous, uneven surface may require a suitable adhesive construction. That does not mean every kraft box needs the same material. Coatings and varnishes can introduce different adhesion questions. Ask the label supplier to name the proposed face stock and adhesive, then test on the actual printed carton.

Design for opening evidence, not shine alone

Decide what the seal is supposed to show when the customer opens the box. Should it tear, delaminate, leave a visible mark, or simply bridge the flap as a visual cue? HERMA's tamper-evident construction overview distinguishes several mechanisms. A generic holographic finish is not the same as a verified tamper-evident structure. If a return-inspection team will use the seal, share an intact sample and an opened sample; the return-inspection guide covers that workflow.

Put the seal across the intended opening seam, with enough contact area on both sides. The placement guide is a starting point, but the actual carton should determine final dimensions. Avoid a placement that can be cut cleanly along the fold while leaving the seal visually intact, or one that lands on an area frequently rubbed by shipping cartons.

Request a small application trial on both finishes

Use the production board, final printing and any varnish or laminate. Apply samples with the planned hand or machine process. Record application temperature, pressure and dwell before shipping simulation or opening. Check edges, flap corners and the actual tear result. Photograph the intact and opened states under normal receiving light. If the kraft and coated cartons behave differently, order separate constructions or adjust the design rather than assuming one seal works everywhere.

The supply format also matters. Hand packing may prefer sheets, while a high-throughput line may require a roll with a specific unwind, liner and gap. The sheet-versus-roll guide can help settle that after the construction is proven on the carton.

What belongs in the custom quote

Include both carton samples, closure drawings, finished printing and coating specifications, seal size, target visual effect, expected opening signal, application method, quantities per design and destination handling route. Ask which properties have been verified on a physical sample and which remain supplier assumptions. Do not call a seal "tamper proof"; no sticker can establish that on its own.
