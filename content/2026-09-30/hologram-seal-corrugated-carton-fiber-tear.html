A holographic strip can look convincing across a carton lid, especially under showroom light. Then someone opens the box and the strip lifts cleanly from the kraft surface, leaving no useful evidence. I would not approve a carton security seal from a flat swatch photo. The real test is on the actual carton, across the actual opening seam, after the adhesive has had the stated time to bond.

Corrugated board is not one consistent surface. Coated cartons, uncoated kraft, printed varnish and exposed recycled fibers offer different bonding conditions. A label that transfers a VOID message on one substrate may remove fibers on another or come away intact on a third. Choose the evidence you want before choosing the shiny finish.

Define what “tamper evident” means on this carton

The buyer needs to specify an observable outcome: carton-fiber damage, a broken label, a transferred pattern or another visible change on attempted removal. Avery Dennison's security-label overview illustrates fiber-tear constructions; its tamper-evident information sheet also shows labels crossing box seams. Those are examples of possible mechanisms, not evidence that every holographic seal on every carton will behave the same way.

Ask whether the desired evidence should remain on the box, on the removed sticker, or both. Think about the buyer receiving the parcel: can they identify a compromised seal quickly, or would ordinary shipping scuffs look the same? This matters more than the complexity of the diffraction pattern. A security feature only helps if someone knows what to inspect.

Place the seal where opening actually breaks it

Use an assembled and filled sample carton. Mark the flap that opens first and the full route the sticker must bridge. Avoid a placement that only touches a decorative outer sleeve while the real flap can be opened from another edge. The seal needs enough bond on both sides of the seam; a narrow strip over a large gap may lose useful contact. Check the fold angle so the material does not tent above the corrugation.

Carton coatings and print finishes belong in the sample request. If the final carton will have varnish or soft-touch laminate, a plain brown sample is not equivalent. Also identify whether packing staff press the seal by hand or use equipment. A die-line that works slowly on a bench may snag at production speed. The placement guide covers other pack geometries.

Run a simple, documented opening trial

Apply candidate seals to several finished cartons using the agreed pressure and environment. Inspect initially, after the specified dwell, after normal shipping handling and when the carton is opened by a person who has not seen the design proof. Photograph the intact seal and the disturbed surface. Record whether the intended evidence was obvious, whether the carton was damaged in a way the brand accepts, and whether legitimate opening became impractically difficult.

This is an acceptance-plan suggestion, not a report of RP's factory results. The AI-generated image above shows a possible fiber-tear outcome; it is not a product-performance claim. If a supplier offers a VOID, destructible and fiber-tear sample, compare each on the finished carton, not on a glass desk. See the VOID versus destructible guide before settling on a mechanism.

What I would approve

Keep the selected physical sample, substrate description, seal dimensions, placement photograph and observed removal behavior together. The quote should state the facestock, adhesive family where available, construction and whether serial numbers or variable QR printing are included. A hologram can add visual distinction, but it does not replace a suitable tamper-evident construction.

Send a finished carton and a photo of its opening path through the custom hologram inquiry. With those in hand, the conversation becomes about a testable seal rather than a generic “anti-counterfeit” sticker.
