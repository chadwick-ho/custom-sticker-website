The front of a supplement bottle is where a brand wants its large name, color block and flavor cue. It is also where the net quantity statement belongs. I often see that line added after the artwork is nearly final, squeezed into the curve at the bottom of a jar or moved onto a side panel because the hero design has filled the space.

For a U.S. dietary supplement, that is not merely a taste question. FDA's general labeling guide identifies the statement of identity and net quantity as front-panel requirements. The brand's regulatory team should decide final wording and compliance, but the packaging buyer can prevent an avoidable space problem before the roll is printed.

Reserve the lower front zone first

FDA's net quantity guide says the statement generally belongs as a distinct item in the bottom 30 percent of the principal display panel, parallel to the package base, with a noted exception for very small principal display panels. It also ties minimum type size to panel area. Do not treat the entire wrap circumference as the front display area; for a cylinder, FDA explains a specific principal-display-panel calculation.

Before selecting a label height, mark the actual usable straight wall on the filled, capped bottle. Draw the front-facing area and a lower-front text reserve. Check whether the bottle has a base curve, shoulder or cap shadow that will eat into it. Then place the brand design around that reserve. This does not require a dull design; it requires admitting that required information is part of the design.

Count what the package actually contains

The net quantity might be a count, weight, measure or a permitted combination, depending on the product. The FDA guide addresses the form of the statement and, for weight or measure, the dual-unit presentation. A sample bottle marked "60 capsules" cannot automatically become a powder tub marked "60 servings" with the same quantity treatment. Have the final pack size and statement approved for each SKU.

Our RP Wellness photo uses fictional quantities and formula copy to show layout. It is not an approved nutrition label. A buyer should replace every illustrative figure with the actual reviewed copy and should test the longest product name and largest package count in the family, not only the easiest flavor.

Look at the bottle, not only the artboard

A flat wrap proof can make the lower-front line look centered while the applied label sits just above a curved base or is partially hidden by a carton window. Ask for an actual-size print on each bottle shape. Put the bottle on a shelf at ordinary viewing height, turn it slowly and check whether the quantity remains distinct from badges, flavor text and decorative rules. If the bottle is square, see whether the front panel wraps across a corner.

I would document the chosen front-view photograph and the applied position. The supplier needs a visual target for production. If an artwork revision pushes the quantity below the straight wall or into a seam, that should trigger a new applied proof, even if the PDF's overall dimensions have not changed.

What to send with a custom-label request

Provide the bottle SKU or dimensioned drawing, capacity and actual contents, final reviewed net quantity line, front-panel direction, label stock, print quantity per variant and method of application. Ask for an assembled proof showing the front and information panel in relation to the cap and base. If there are multiple bottle sizes, request a separate fit check for each.

When price comparisons arrive, compare the same approved copy, die size and material. A cheaper quote for a label that cannot place the quantity cleanly is not actually the cheaper option. Get the regulatory decision and the physical fit decision signed off before releasing the roll.
